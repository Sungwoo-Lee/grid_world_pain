# Dreamer imagination dream-strip: tooling + contact-anchoring method

## Key conclusion
`scripts/dreamer/visualize_dream.py` generates the Dreamer imagination "dream-strip" figure — a 7-row, event-centered strip (real scene, ground truth, real-action open-loop rollout, one-step prediction, policy actions, and `WorldModel.imagine()` policy-action rollout). Its default event anchor (t=0) is a **nociception rising edge** (pain onset). To study whether the world model *anticipates* a collision, pin t=0 to ONE STEP BEFORE the agent contacts the animal via `--event-step = contact_step - 1`; the imagination rows then reveal whether the dream predicts the imminent contact before it happens.

## Evidence, measurements, facts
- Required args: `--model-config <run>/models/agent_config.yaml --episode <.rec.gz> --checkpoint-dir <run>/checkpoints/ --checkpoint-step N --event-step E --pre-steps P --horizon H --out-dir`. Output PNGs land deep under `<recdir>/../../videos/<ckpt>/`; collect them into a clean tree.
- Contact detection from the recording's `snapshots` list (each dict has `agent_pos`, `animal_pos`, `injury_level`): contact = first step where Chebyshev(agent_pos, animal_pos)==0. Snapshots align 1:1 with obs steps (snapshots truncate at episode end; obs is padded to 101).
- **Threat-vs-neutral contrast, empirically**: in pred conditions, same-cell contact coincides exactly with an injury rise (predator collision = damage); in rabbit conditions the agent also walks onto the rabbit's cell but injury never rises (harmless) and it survives the full episode. So predator contact is nociceptive, rabbit contact is not.
- Timing sanity: `--event-step 20` -> the real action at t=0 is `actions_all[21]` (the move INTO the predator at step 21), confirming t=0 sits one step before contact.
- Ran on b04_128env at ckpt 380002: 8 figures (pred_inj00 x3, pred_inj70 x1, rabbit_inj00 x3, rabbit_inj70 x1) into `results/eval/avoidance/dream_viz/b04_128env/`. The script hard-labels the t=0 column "(damage)"; here damage actually lands at t+1.
