# Our replay_ratio vs DreamerV3's train_ratio: same quantity, different unit (x batch*seq)

## Key conclusion
Verified directly against the DreamerV3 paper PDF that our `replay_ratio` and the paper's `train_ratio` measure the same thing in different units. **train_ratio = number of replayed steps (frames) per policy step**; **our replay_ratio = gradient steps per policy step**. Since one gradient step replays `batch_size x batch_length = 16 x 64 = 1024` frames, **train_ratio = replay_ratio x 1024**. Our replay-ratio sweep `{1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.0625}` therefore maps to train_ratio `{1024, 512, 256, 64}` — spanning DreamerV3's **entire tuned range**, from its most data-scarce setting (Atari-100k / BSuite = 1024) to its data-rich setting (Atari-200M / DMLab = 64). The paper tunes train_ratio *inversely* with env count / data budget.

## Evidence, measurements, facts
- Primary source: `docs/project/references/Dreamer/sources/Hafner et al. 2023 - Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models.pdf`.
  - Table A.1 caption (p.18): "The train ratio is the number of replayed steps per policy steps rather than environment steps, and thus unaware of the action repeat."
  - Table A.1 values: Atari-100k 1 env / train_ratio **1024**; DMC 4 env / 512; Crafter 1 env / 512; Atari-200M 8 env / **64**; DMLab 8 env / 64; Minecraft 16 env / **16**.
  - Table W.1 (p.38): fixed across all suites — Batch size B **16**, Batch length T **64**, Imagination horizon H **15**, Replay capacity 10^6.
- Our config: `per_rank_batch_size: 16`, `per_rank_sequence_length: 64` => exact `x1024` conversion (1024 <-> replay_ratio 1.0).
- Our `replay_ratio` shares the UNIT of SR-SPR's UTD ratio (gradient updates per env-step), NOT DreamerV3's frame-based train_ratio; magnitudes are not comparable across model-free (SR-SPR) vs model-based (Dreamer) because "one update" does different work.
