# Cover-seeking is a late-emerging skill: early model runs, mature model hides

## Key conclusion
Running the same avoidance probe on an **early checkpoint** (`randpred`, 1.5M steps) vs the mature subject (8.9M) shows two different avoidance *strategies*: the early agent **flees by running a lap around the grid perimeter (kiting)** with the predator one cell behind, and almost never uses the bush; the mature agent **dives into the bush and hides**. So **cover-seeking (use the bush as a refuge that breaks tracking) is a late-emerging skill** — absent at 1.5M even though the reward curve is flattening. The *form* of avoidance keeps maturing after the reward plateaus.

## Evidence, measurements, facts
- Early model: `20260630-051225_rppo_basic05_randpred_n110`, ckpt `models/1500007` (~1.5M). Differs from the mature `randinit` subject ONLY in that the training predator's dynamics are randomized per episode (`move_interval [1,3]`, `attack_delay [1,3]`, `detection_range [1,7]` vs fixed `1/3/3`) — a weaker, variable predator.
- Predator condition, mature vs early: entered-bush 100% vs 13-23%; time-in-bush 20-33% vs ~1%; steps-to-reach-bush 6 vs ~56-65 (late/incidental); spatial spread R_g 3.9-4.4 vs 4.9-5.0 (early roams more).
- Trajectory (early, pred_inj00): predator reaches adjacency ~t3; agent then runs down→across the bottom→up the far side→along the top to a corner (predator trailing at distance 1), shaking it only at the corner. Never touches the bush.
- **Metric-validity finding**: bush-use metrics are the decisive run-vs-hide discriminators; `time moving (%)` (~65% both) and `longest chase (steps)` (~29 both) are near-FLAT — because the mature model hides *intermittently* (hide→emerge→flee→re-hide), so it moves a lot too and the predator repeatedly closes to ≤2 cells. So "run vs hide" is a **cover-USE distinction, not a gross-mobility one** (counter to the initial hypothesis that movement metrics would separate them).

## Decisions and actions
- Generated journal-style heatmaps for both models from the reusable script; videos collected for the early model showing the perimeter-run.
- Keep `spatial spread (R_g)` as a graded roaming measure; `time moving` / `longest chase` are low-value here (would matter more in open-arena probes without a bush).

## Open questions and follow-ups
- Re-run when `randpred` finishes training to see whether bush-cover behavior emerges (and whether the weaker training predator, not just training stage, suppresses cover-seeking).
- Disentangle "early stage" from "weaker training predator" as the cause of the run-not-hide strategy.
