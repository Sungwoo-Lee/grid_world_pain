Does the Modulator Listen Inward?

Sixteen agents are training right now, differing in what a small second network is allowed to read and which parts of the policy it is allowed to re-tune. This is the plan for reading them — what will be measured, why those measures and not the obvious one, and what would have to be true for the answer to be believed. The results section at the end is deliberately empty.

The question, and the measure that cannot answer it

The agent carries a second, small network — the neuromodulator — that reads its senses and continuously re-tunes the main policy, the way a brain chemical such as acetylcholine re-tunes cortex. The claim the project is built on is not that a modulated agent behaves differently. It is sharper than that: that a modulated agent's response to the outside world should vary more with its internal state — that being hurt should change how it reads a predator, not merely change what it does.

That is an interaction, not a main effect, and it is what everything below is built to measure.

The one thing that changes what you asked for

You asked for an internal-state-dependent bush hiding comparison. Bush hiding is the measure this project has used for a year, and a recent analysis showed it is largely a by-product rather than a decision.

A wounded agent mostly freezes to heal. Its Rest rate rises by 17.7 to 35.2 percentage points — about five times the size of the bush-hiding effect — and that resting happens mostly in the open. Whether freezing registers as “hides more” depends only on whether the agent happened to be standing on a bush when it stopped. Across fourteen agents, “hides more when injured” was positive in 13 of 14 over the first 25 steps, 2 of 14 over the first 100, and 1 of 14 over the whole episode. The sign is a property of the window and of where cover happens to be, not of a decision.

So bush hiding stays in the battery — it is the historical measure and dropping it would break comparability with every earlier study — but it is demoted. It cannot carry the headline, and a modulator that changes it has not yet been shown to have changed anything the agent decided.

The escape is to score a movement decision instead of a location. An agent that freezes then contributes nothing in either direction: freezing removes the step from the denominator rather than loading it into the numerator. That measure already exists, is already pre-registered for this grid, and is described in the battery below as B0.

Words this page uses

Every term below appears later on. Open the ones you need; skip the rest. Nothing here is required reading if the vocabulary is already familiar.

The agent and its body

Interoceptive — sensing your own body from the inside

Interoception is the sense of your own internal condition: hunger, pain, how hard your heart is working. It is the counterpart of exteroception, which is sensing the outside world through eyes, ears and nose. This agent has both. Its interoceptive channels report how injured it is, how hungry it is, and a pain-like signal; its exteroceptive channels report what it can see, smell and bump into.

The whole project is named for this distinction, and this experiment is the first that can switch one off and leave the other on.

Nociception, and why it is not called pain — the raw damage signal

Nociception is the detection of tissue damage — the signal a nerve sends when something harmful happens. Pain is the experience that may or may not follow from it. Keeping the two apart is not pedantry here: pain is the thing this project is trying to explain, so calling the input “pain” would assume the conclusion. The agent has a nociceptor; whether anything pain-like emerges is the open question.

The agent's nociceptor is also delayed and smoothed. It does not report the wound directly; it reports a weighted average of the last twelve steps of damage, which means an agent that wakes up badly hurt feels nothing at all for two steps and does not feel the full wound until step twelve. Several findings in this project turn on that delay.

Bush hiding — the fraction of steps spent standing in cover

The world contains bushes that conceal the agent from predators. Bush hiding is simply the share of an episode's steps the agent spent standing in one. It has been this project's main behavioural measure for about a year, which is why it appears here at all — and why the finding that it is mostly a by-product of freezing, rather than a decision to hide, mattered enough to change this plan's headline measure.

Survival steps — how long the agent stayed alive

Performance in this project is always measured in survival steps, never in accumulated reward. Reward is what the agent is trained on; survival is what we actually care about, and the two can come apart. An agent can learn to farm reward in ways that do not keep it alive longer.

The modulator

Neuromodulator (the “NMN”) — a small network that re-tunes the big one

The agent's decisions come from one main network. Alongside it sits a much smaller second network that reads the agent's senses and, at every step, adjusts how the main network processes what it sees. The biological analogy is a neuromodulator such as acetylcholine or noradrenaline: a chemical that does not carry the message itself but changes how the rest of the brain handles it.

The point is not that a modulated agent does different things. It is that a modulated agent should respond to the same outside cue differently depending on its internal state — that being hurt should change how it reads a predator.

FiLM — the specific way the re-tuning is done

Short for Feature-wise Linear Modulation. For every neuron in the layer being modulated, the modulator produces two numbers: a gain to multiply that neuron's output by, and an offset to add to it. That is the entire operation — multiply and add, per neuron.

It matters here because until this week different modulation sites in this codebase used different operations, so “where the modulator acts” was tangled up with “how it acts”. A companion refactor made every site use FiLM, which is what finally allows the two to be varied separately.

Site, and the five row labels — which part of the policy gets re-tuned

The main network is a pipeline: senses go in, get encoded, pass through a memory cell, and come out as an action choice and a value estimate. The modulator can be attached at any of those places, and the grid's rows are exactly that choice.

t2enc re-tunes the sensory front-end, where raw senses are first processed. t3rnn re-tunes the memory cell, which carries information across steps. t4act re-tunes the action layers, which choose what to do. t5crt re-tunes the value head, which estimates how good the situation is. t16quad re-tunes all four at once, and t1none is the control with no modulator at all.

I, X and ALL — which senses the modulator is allowed to read

I is interoceptive only: the modulator sees the body's own channels — injury, hunger, the nociceptive signal — and nothing of the outside world. X is exteroceptive only: sight, smell, collision, and nothing of the body. ALL is everything, which is what the modulator has always read until now.

This is the axis the project's thesis rests on, which is why the results section leads with the I-versus-X comparison rather than with a ranking of sites.

How the measuring works

Percentage points (pp) — why not just “percent”

If a rate goes from 20% to 25%, that is a rise of five percentage points, but a rise of twenty-five percent relative to where it started. Both are true and they are different numbers. This page always uses percentage points for changes in a rate, so that a change is never confused with a proportion of a change.

Randomised starting injury — and why it is the only honest thing to bin on

At the start of every episode the environment deals the agent a wound drawn at random, before the agent has done anything. That number is causally identified: because it was assigned at random, any behaviour that tracks it must have been caused by it.

Contrast the injury the agent happens to be carrying at some later step. That is partly a consequence of the agent's own choices — a cautious agent gets hurt less — so a correlation between carried injury and behaviour could run in either direction. Both quantities get measured in this project, but only the randomised one supports a causal claim, and the two are kept carefully apart.

Seed, and why one is not enough — the limit that governs this whole analysis

A seed is the starting number for the random-number generator: it fixes the initial network weights and the order training data arrives in. Two runs of identical configuration with different seeds are two samples from the same distribution, and they can land far apart.

In this project they land very far apart. Five runs of one identical configuration in the immediately preceding study finished anywhere between 35.5 and 136.7 survival steps. Every cell in this grid has one seed, so a gap between two cells could be the modulator or could be the dice, and nothing in the data separates them.

Pre-registration — deciding the test before seeing the data

Writing down what will be measured, and what counts as a positive result, before looking at any of the numbers. It exists because a threshold chosen after seeing the data is not a test: with enough measures and enough freedom to pick, something always looks significant.

It is why this page's results section is empty and must stay empty. Once a number from this grid has been looked at, nothing in the plan above may be revised without a dated note saying what changed and why, with the original left visible.

Episode, and checkpoint — one life, and a saved snapshot

An episode is one life: the agent spawns into a freshly randomised world, acts until it dies or runs out of time, and the world resets. Training here runs for ten million of them.

A checkpoint is a saved copy of the network partway through training. They are written periodically, so a run that has reached 9.4 million episodes has roughly forty-seven of them. Comparing two agents at different checkpoints compares different amounts of training as well as different designs, which is why nothing here is computed until every cell has reached the same one.

Rest and Eat — the two actions that are not movement

The agent chooses one action per step. Four of them are moves. The other two are Rest, which holds still and lets injury heal, and Eat, which consumes food on the current square.

They matter disproportionately here. A wounded agent rests far more, and that single fact is what makes location-based measures untrustworthy: an agent that stops moving stops changing where it is, so any measure of where it is starts reporting how much it rested. The headline measure excludes both actions for exactly this reason.

Confound — two explanations the data cannot separate

A confound is a second explanation for a result that the measurement cannot rule out. If injured agents both rest more and sit in bushes more, then “injury causes hiding” and “injury causes resting, and resting happens to occur in bushes” predict the same observation, and the observation cannot tell them apart.

Most of the design decisions on this page are attempts to remove one specific confound, and most of the limits section is an admission of the ones that remain.

Quasi-binomial regression — the statistics behind the factor analysis

A way of modelling a proportion — here, the fraction of an episode's steps spent in cover — against several explanatory variables at once, so that the effect of each can be read with the others held fixed.

The quasi part matters: ordinary binomial regression assumes a specific amount of random scatter, and real episodes scatter much more than that. Left uncorrected, that makes every effect look far more certain than it is. The quasi-binomial version measures the excess scatter from the data and widens its error bars accordingly — in this project by a factor of thirteen to twenty-seven.

The trajectory store, and its row footgun — where the data lives

After training, each agent is put back into the exact world it trained in and run for a large number of episodes, and everything it did is recorded into a store of files — one row per step, plus a row for the starting state.

The footgun: row t holds the state of the world at t together with the action that arrived at t. So the action a row carries is the one that produced it, not the one chosen from it. Getting that off by one silently measures a different quantity — and has done, twice, in this project's history. Every measure on this page states which row it reads from for that reason.

What is training

Two things about the modulator have never been varied independently, because until this week the code did not allow it: what it reads, and which parts of the policy it re-tunes. A companion refactor made every re-tuning site use the same operation and made each site switchable, so “where it acts” is finally separable from “how it acts”. The grid crosses the two factors.

Why the purple column is the point

If a modulator that reads only the body produces internal-state-dependent behaviour, the project's story holds: an interoceptive signal re-tunes perception. If a modulator that reads only the outside world does just as well, the story is in trouble — whatever the modulator is doing, it is not interoception, and the same benefit was available without ever looking inward. That single contrast is worth more than any ranking of sites.

The measure battery

Four tiers, and the tier matters as much as the measure. Tier A is pre-registered: its threshold was fixed before any run in this grid existed and may not be adjusted afterwards. Everything below it is either a decomposition that explains a tier-A result or an exploratory measure that may generate a hypothesis but may not settle one.

Every measure is computed per cell and compared against the unmodulated control.

What B0 actually is

The exact computation — open if you will be checking or reproducing this

Written out because the store's row convention has bitten this project before — row t holds the world at t together with the action that arrived at t, so an entry event straddles two rows:

denominator:  agent_in_bush[t]   == False        # standing in the open
              action[t+1]        in {0,1,2,3}    # and chose to MOVE (not Rest, not Eat)

numerator:    agent_in_bush[t+1] == True         # the move landed in cover

binned by:    the injury the environment dealt at t = 0, before the agent acted
              -- the only internal-state variable that is causally identified

Binning on the randomised starting injury rather than the injury the agent happens to be carrying is what makes the comparison causal rather than associational. An agent's carried injury is partly a consequence of its own behaviour; the wound it was dealt at reset is not.

In one sentence, without the notation: of the times the agent was standing in the open and decided to take a step, how often did that step put it into cover — and does that fraction change depending on how badly it woke up hurt?

What would make this wrong

Stated before any number exists, because a limit disclosed after a result reads as an excuse.

One seed per cell — the limit that governs everything else

Sixteen cells at one seed each gives a number for every combination and a confidence interval for none. The immediately preceding study ran five seeds of one identical configuration and they finished between 35.5 and 136.7 survival steps. A gap of that size between two cells of this grid could be the modulator or could be the seed, and nothing in the data distinguishes them.

The consequence is asymmetric, and it is the part worth internalising: a cell that wins is protected, because the design requires it to be re-run at five seeds before anyone may call it an effect. A cell that loses is not protected. A site that would work fine, handed one unlucky seed, gets recorded as “does not help” and dropped — and nobody re-runs a negative. This analysis will therefore report no eliminations. A cell that fails to clear a threshold will be reported as untested, not as refuted.

Four narrower threats, each with what is actually done about it

  • Thirteen of sixteen runs are still training. Comparing a cell stopped at 8.2 million episodes against one that reached 10 million confounds the modulator with the budget. Nothing is computed until every cell has reached the same checkpoint, and the checkpoint step used is reported per cell rather than assumed equal.
  • The store's identity hash is not a same-world check. It hashes the whole resolved config including the seed and the run tag, so it differs across runs whose environment is identical. A reader who treats it as a pairing check will wrongly conclude the comparison is invalid. Pairing is established empirically instead — by confirming the animal draws, observation draws, starting injury and spawn cell are bit-identical across shared episode seeds.
  • One action index cannot be verified from the store. B0's denominator excludes both Rest and Eat. Rest has a witness column and is checked on every store; Eat does not, and is taken from the source. If the action ordering ever changed, Rest would fail loudly and Eat would fail silently. Stated because it is not fixable from the data, only bounded.
  • A dead modulator and a useless one look identical. This is what tier C is for. Without the modulator's own internal statistics, “this site does not help” cannot be separated from “this site never learned to do anything at all”, and the second is a training result rather than a scientific one.

An open question that is yours, not mine

The experiment's own reviewer proposed spending the same sixteen slots differently: five write sites at three seeds each, then the two survivors crossed with the input slices at three seeds. That buys replication on every comparison — eliminations become trustworthy — at the cost of deferring the interoception question, the one the project's thesis rests on, by roughly fifteen hours. The grid as launched answers the thesis question immediately and cannot eliminate anything. Both are defensible; the choice is a research call rather than a technical one, and it is recorded in the design document as still open.

What gets reported, and in what order

Fixed now so that the reporting order cannot be chosen after seeing which cut looks best.

  1. Does the modulator do anything at all? Every cell against the unmodulated control, on B0 first and survival second.
  2. Body-only against world-only. The purple column against the blue one, pooled across sites. This is the thesis test and it is reported whichever way it comes out.
  3. Which site, if any. Ranked, with every difference smaller than the seed-noise floor explicitly marked as indistinguishable rather than ordered.
  4. The decomposition. Rest rate and the two halves of bush hiding, so a headline movement can be attributed to a decision or to freezing.
  5. The modulator's own behaviour. Tier C, used only to explain a null — never to rescue one.
  6. Exploratory. Tier D, labelled as such, generating hypotheses for a replicated follow-up.

Results

Empty on purpose — no run has been read

Thirteen of the sixteen cells are still training as this page is written, and the plan above fixes what will be measured before the numbers exist. That ordering is the only thing that makes a pre-registered threshold worth anything, so this section stays empty until every cell has reached the same checkpoint.

When it is filled, it will carry: the six reports above in that order; a per-cell table with the checkpoint step each number was computed at; the empirical pairing check; the Rest-versus-movement guard result for every store; and, for any comparison smaller than the seed-noise floor, the words indistinguishable at one seed rather than a rank.

Nothing in the plan above may be revised once a number from this grid has been looked at. If something here turns out to be wrong — as the bush-hiding measure did in the study before this one — the revision gets its own dated note saying what changed and why, and the original stays visible.
