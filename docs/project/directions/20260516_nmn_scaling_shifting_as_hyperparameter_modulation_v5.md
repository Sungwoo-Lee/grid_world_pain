# NMN as scaling-and-shifting implementation of RL-hyperparameter modulation (v5)

## 1. What this direction proposes (plain-English entry point)

This direction memo proposes that the project's small **modulator network** — the NMN, a side network that watches the agent's internal state and emits two signals (a multiplicative scale γ and an additive shift β) into three places inside the main policy via a **FiLM layer** (feature-wise linear modulation: $h \mapsto \gamma(c) \odot h + \beta(c)$) — computationally instantiates a **single principle drawn from biology's noradrenergic system**: one upstream signal, multiplied by target-specific readout matrices, produces a *basket of downstream behavioural-knob-like effects* simultaneously across the agent. The paper does not claim every neuromodulator paper fits FiLM; it does not claim FiLM is *the* substrate for biological neural gain. It claims the *possibility* of a bridge — a single computational demonstration that the principle "one signal, many target-specific effects" can be built and run end-to-end.

**v5 names the targeted biological system explicitly: noradrenaline / locus coeruleus (NA / LC).** The project's empirical anchor — the modulator-augmented agent recovered roughly 25× the seed-noise floor faster than the unmodulated baseline on the second return to a previously-seen task stage, with the modulator's `temperature_mean` swinging sign-consistently at three of four stage transitions ([memory insight 20260513_0014](../../../.claude-memory/memories/nmn_diagnosis/20260513_0014_nmn_r2_continual_h1b_h1c_confirmed_high_margin.md)) — is the canonical phasic-burst-at-regime-change signature predicted by the Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005 adaptive-gain theory of LC-NA. Targeting one system makes the paper *interpretable* to biological reviewers; acknowledging one-to-many (LC-NA produces a basket of downstream effects, not a single hyperparameter) makes it *honest*; deferring full coverage of acetylcholine (ACh), dopamine (DA), and serotonin (5-HT) to future work keeps the scope bounded. v5 absorbs the 20260516 impactful-vs-reasonable symposium's load-bearing convergence — *substrate is right, rhetoric needs softening one level further* — and applies the user's refined constraint consistently: **target one system, produce one focused result, acknowledge that the targeted system fans out into multiple effects, defer the rest**.

**Headline finding (the paper's central claim).** A single LC-NA-analog gain signal, fed through three target-specific FiLM readouts on a recurrent PPO policy, produces a *basket of downstream behavioural-hyperparameter-like effects* on an interoceptive RL agent. The three injection sites are not three channels carrying three hyperparameters; they are three *targets* on which the same upstream signal acts through three different readout matrices, with each target producing several effect-flavours simultaneously. The paper demonstrates the *possibility* of this bridge between neural-gain biology and behavioural-hyperparameter RL theory; it does not verify its completeness.

## 3. The unification claim

**The project targets noradrenergic / locus-coeruleus modulation as its biological anchor.** The R2 anchor's phasic burst at regime change — the modulator's `temperature_mean` swinging sign-consistently at three of four stage transitions, with the modulator-augmented agent recovering roughly 25× the seed-noise floor faster than the unmodulated baseline on return-to-active stages — is the canonical LC-NA signature in the adaptive-gain theory of Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005. Targeting one system explicitly is non-negotiable under the user's refined constraint: a paper that names no biological target is uninterpretable to biological reviewers, and the project's downstream identity in pain modelling does not survive an "unspecified neuromodulator" framing. NA / LC is the natural target the empirical anchor points to.

**Each injection site is a *target* of the LC-NA-analog modulator, not a channel of it.** v4 wrote *"Site A (encoder pre-fusion) — α channel; ACh-as-learning-rate"* and *"Site C (policy logits) — β channel; NA-as-inverse-temperature"*. The 20260516 symposium's neuromodulation contribution flagged this as the same one-to-one error v4 just called out in Doya's four-channel framework, applied one level down at the per-site level. v5 drops the per-site channel-attribution rhetoric. In its place: each site is *one target* on which the single LC-NA-analog modulator acts through a *target-specific FiLM readout matrix*, producing a *basket of effects* via that readout. The receptor-density analogy makes this concrete — a single LC-NA projection to a cortical region produces heterogeneous downstream effects via the layer-specific and cell-type-specific adrenergic receptor density at that region (Ferguson & Cardin 2020 *Neuromodulatory control*; Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005; Sara 2009). The FiLM readout matrix is the in-silico analogue.

Concretely, the three injection sites carry three target-and-basket labels:

- **Site A (encoder pre-fusion) — the perception target.** The basket of effects under one LC-NA-analog signal includes learning-rate-like effects (Doya 2002 §3.4), sensory-precision-like effects (Friston 2010; Adams, Shipp & Friston 2013), divisive-normalisation-like effects (Ferguson & Cardin 2020 Box 1d), and expected-uncertainty-like effects (Yu & Dayan 2005). All of these are *simultaneously plausible* readings of what the modulator does at A; the substrate is *not committed* to one of them.
- **Site B (GRU update gate) — the memory target.** The basket of effects includes plasticity-gating (per-unit hidden-state retention, $\tau_h$), state-transition-precision-like effects (Adams, Shipp & Friston 2013), memory-consolidation-like effects (Sara 2009 NA literature; Hasselmo ACh literature), and prior-precision-elevation-like effects (Büchel et al. 2014). Note that **plasticity gating is *not* γ_Bellman**: the GRU update gate is a per-unit retention quantity bounded by $(0,1)$, while Bellman γ is a global scalar inside a temporal sum. The math-reviewer audit of the underlying concept memo ([film_neuromod_integration.md §10 flag (d)](../concepts/film_neuromod_integration.md)) ruled the v3 conflation dimensionally incoherent; v5 inherits the v4 correction.
- **Site C (policy logits) — the policy target.** The basket of effects includes temperature-like effects (NA inverse temperature; Doya 2002 §3.3; Lee 2024 §3.4 Eq. 4), action-prior-shift effects (additive β at C; the rheobase-on-policy analogue), attentional-bias effects (Sarter & Lustig; Aston-Jones), policy-precision-like effects (the active-inference reading of inverse temperature on expected free energy; Pezzulo, Rigoli & Friston 2015), and exploration-bonus-like effects (Aston-Jones tonic-vs-phasic exploration / exploitation arbitration).

The **fourth Doya channel (5-HT / γ_Bellman)** is *out of the forward-pass FiLM substrate* — the same boundary Lee 2024 §6 honestly drew. A forward-pass FiLM operator produces per-feature affine on activations; γ_Bellman is a scalar inside a temporal sum at the loss / TD-target layer. v5 preserves the v4 §5.9 boundary unchanged and adds one new boundary under the refined constraint: *full coverage of ACh + DA + 5-HT systems is out of scope of this paper and named as future work*.

**Scalar-to-population bridge.** `mod_h` is the modulator's single recurrent state, biologically analogous to "one nucleus, one tone" — and the per-site FiLM readout matrices are the receptor-density-analog fan-outs that turn that single scalar into per-feature γ, β across each target. Per-feature $(\gamma, \beta) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the population dimension biology already commits to (Ferguson & Cardin 2020). One signal, three targets, three different readouts, multiple effects at each target.

**The substrate is *natively one-to-many*.** The Galanti & Wolf 2020 modularity theorem (Theorem 4) gives the formal identifiability anchor: a hypernetwork's parameter-efficiency bound $N_g = O(\epsilon^{-m_1/r})$ vs embedding-concatenation $\Omega(\epsilon^{-(m_1+m_2)/r})$ comes from *the read-out matrices carrying the per-target specificity while the conditioner is shared*. **The dissociation lives in the read-out, not in the signal.** A sceptical reviewer asking "if one signal produces three dissociable effects at three targets, what *are* the three effects in the signal itself?" has a formal answer: nothing — the signal is low-dimensional; the per-target effects are produced by the per-target readouts; this is exactly the architectural pattern hypernetworks exist for. The same theorem also says: one signal, multiple per-target effects via target-specific readouts is the *expected* architectural form, not a workaround.

### §3.0 Family-tree placement of the substrate (lineage callout)

The investigation memo on FiLM ⊂ Hypernet ⊂ Bayesian Hypernet vs BNN ([film_hypernet_bnn_lineage_and_critic_modulation.md §2](../concepts/film_hypernet_bnn_lineage_and_critic_modulation.md)) makes five formal lineage claims that v5 inherits. In compact form:

1. **FiLM ⊂ Hypernet (rigorous).** A FiLM layer is a hypernetwork whose generated "weights" are restricted to per-channel diagonal scaling plus a constant translation. Independent routes through Ha 2016 §3.2 and Abdollahzadeh 2021. The project's three-site FiLM substrate **is** a hypernetwork; the modulator is its generator $\phi$.
2. **Hypernet ⊆ point-mass BHN (rigorous, as a *limit*).** A deterministic hypernetwork is the limiting case of a Bayesian Hypernetwork (BHN; Krueger 2017) whose implicit posterior over weights collapses to a point mass: $q_\phi(W \mid c) = \delta(W - g_\phi(c))$.
3. **BNN ⊥ BHN (rigorous, orthogonal).** A Bayesian Neural Network (Kendall & Gal 2017 in the project's corpus) places a posterior on the *main network's leaf weights*; a Bayesian Hypernetwork places a posterior on the *generator*, inducing context-conditional weight distributions. The two architectures are not nested.
4. **FiLM-Ensemble ≈ finite-mixture BHN approximation (holds with caveat).** Turkoglu 2022's FiLM-Ensemble has predictive distribution equal to a Monte Carlo estimate of $\mathbb{E}_q[\sigma(f_{\theta,\gamma,\beta}(x))]$ under a discrete categorical posterior; with the caveat that members are trained jointly under one objective, not sampled from a learned posterior. Honest framing: **finite-mixture approximation of a posterior over FiLM scalars**.
5. **Galanti & Wolf 2020 Thm 4 modularity carries through (rigorous).** The hypernetwork modularity bound applies to every member of the family — FiLM, deterministic-Hypernet, BHN, and FiLM-Ensemble all inherit the same parameter-efficient capacity allocation **and** the same one-conditioner-many-per-target-readouts identifiability structure.

**Architectural grounding for one-to-many readouts.** Theorem 4's parameter-efficiency comes from the read-out matrices carrying the per-target specificity while the conditioner is shared. The modulator signal does *not* need to be decomposed into per-target channels for the architecture to produce dissociable per-target effects. The dissociation lives in the read-out, not in the signal. This identifiability reading of Theorem 4 — separate from its standard capacity reading — is the formal load-bearing anchor for the substrate's one-to-many claim under the user's refined constraint.

**Where the project's substrate sits.** Today: deterministic, context-conditional. By Claim 1 the substrate is a hypernetwork; by Claim 2 it is the point-mass-BHN limit; by Claim 3 it is orthogonal to BNN; by Claim 5 it inherits both the capacity bound and the identifiability structure. The compound formula in [film_neuromod_integration.md §5.4 Row EE-6](../concepts/film_neuromod_integration.md) — FiLM-Ensemble + Kendall-Gal heteroscedastic precision on the modulator's output — is the architectural upgrade that moves the substrate one row down in the family toward stochastic context-conditional weights, and by Claim 4 it is **the first instantiation of an approximate Bayesian Hypernetwork with a heteroscedastic likelihood for an RL policy**: cleanly positioned against Krueger 2017 (full BHN; no heteroscedastic head; not RL) and Kendall & Gal 2017 (BNN; no context-conditioning; not RL).

**Differentiation from Rodriguez-Garcia 2026.** Rodriguez-Garcia modulates the **gradient** (optimiser-level — gain reparameterises learning rate as $\lambda \to \lambda / g^2$); this project modulates the **forward activation** (PPO's Adam untouched). Both invoke noradrenergic gain at orthogonal points; the two could combine later. v5 keeps the v4 sharpening of this contrast: Rodriguez-Garcia leaves the forward-pass substrate for a *gradient-level* gain channel; this project does not leave the substrate at all — it accepts the substrate's boundary (γ_Bellman is out) and acknowledges it rather than architecting around it.

**What `mod_h` is likely doing today.** Under the single-recurrent-state design, `mod_h` is most likely an LC-NA-analog signal — phasic burst at regime change, sign-consistent — with passenger heads at sites A and B fed by the same `mod_h`. Under the refined constraint this reading carries an explicit caveat: the LC-NA-analog signal at site C is not "the NA channel"; it is *one effect of one biological exemplar* the modulator may be approximating.

**Prerequisite — the T/P split.** The multi-effect-at-multiple-targets claim is non-identifiable without the **Phase 0 T/P split** (two recurrent states with separable phasic and tonic timescales). A single `mod_h` forces correlated movement across knobs biology dissociates by timescale (phasic LC bursts at regime change vs. tonic LC tone over minutes; Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005). This memo escalates the T/P split from open question to prerequisite, as v4 did.

**The substrate is *natively one-to-many* (Galanti & Wolf 2020 Theorem 4). One signal, multiple effects at multiple targets, with the read-outs encoding target specificity.**

### §3.5 Interpretive lenses (new)

The user's refined constraint pushes v5 to a structural commitment v4 lacked: the *substrate* (one LC-NA-analog signal, three target-specific FiLM readouts, baskets of effects at each target) is the load-bearing claim. **Multiple interpretive lenses can be applied to the same substrate, and none of them is the "right" one.** The paper presents the substrate first and the lenses second, with the lenses positioned as *complementary readings of the same data*, not as competing structural claims the project commits to. The four professors' contributions to the 20260516 symposium each surface one of these lenses; v5 lists them in parallel.

**Lens 1 — Doya-channel lens (Doya 2002; Lee 2024).** The classical RL-hyperparameter mapping. ACh-α at the perception target, NA-β at the policy target, plasticity gating at the memory target. v5 cites this lens as *one* interpretive frame on the data, useful for situating the project within the Doya-Lee lineage, *not* as the load-bearing structural commitment. The empirical signatures Doya predicts (per-site clamp magnitudes; |δ|-tracking by the policy-target γ arm; effective-rank reduction) are measured under v5; the *interpretation* of those signatures as "ACh implements α" / "NA implements β" is downgraded from claim to lens.

**Lens 2 — Precision-coding lens (Friston 2010; Adams, Shipp & Friston 2013; Kanai et al. 2015; Shine 2021 Box 3).** The hierarchical-predictive-coding reading. The three injection sites read as three precision read-outs from one upstream `mod_h`: sensory precision $\Pi_s$ at the perception target, state-transition precision $\Pi_z$ at the memory target, policy precision $\Pi_\pi$ at the policy target. *One signal, many precisions, no commitment to which biological neuromodulator implements which precision level.* This lens is uniquely fit for the user's two constraints (possibility-not-coverage; no one-to-one) because the precision-coding framework *starts* from one-signal-many-effects as a structural property. The Bayesian-brain professor's symposium contribution argues this is the *natural* articulation of the user's intuition; v5 surfaces it as one lens of four, not as the headline.

**Lens 3 — Hypernet / BHN lens (Galanti & Wolf 2020; Krueger 2017; Kendall & Gal 2017; Turkoglu 2022).** The architectural-lineage reading. The three sites are three observation points on the effect of one side-signal acting through three different read-out matrices; the dissociation profile is a behavioural fingerprint produced by per-target readouts, not a channel decomposition. Galanti & Wolf 2020 Theorem 4 (in its identifiability reading) is the formal anchor. Row EE-6 (FiLM-Ensemble + heteroscedastic precision) is the project's architectural artefact under this lens: an approximate Bayesian Hypernetwork with heteroscedastic likelihood for an RL policy, with the ensemble approximating a posterior over per-target effects rather than recovering separable channels. The RL / Bayesian-DL professor's symposium contribution argues this lens can lead the paper (Framing C2 in their contribution); v5 surfaces it as one lens of four.

**Lens 4 — Target-flexibility lens (Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005; Sara 2009; Ferguson & Cardin 2020; Marder 2012).** The substrate-side biological reading. The modulator's effects are jointly distributed over perceptual, mnemonic, and policy targets via a common slow state; the dissociation between sites lives in their receptor-density-analog readout matrices, not in separable channels of `mod_h` itself. This is *biology's reality* — one nucleus (LC), many downstream effects, target-specificity through receptor density. v5 surfaces this lens as the explicit grounding of the substrate-as-receptor-density-analog framing in §3 above; the neuromodulation professor's symposium contribution (recommendation B.3 + B.2) is the source.

**These are four lenses on the same substrate, not four competing structural claims.** The substrate does not have to commit to one — it has to produce *data the lenses can be tested against*. The §5 prediction program is the test program: each subsection's measurements are interpretable through more than one lens, with the lenses agreeing on the prediction in some places and disagreeing in others. Where they disagree, the data discriminates; where they agree, the project gains breadth across audience without committing to any one framework.

**Implication for the rhetoric of the rest of the memo.** v5 §5 names predictions and signatures in *target-and-basket language* (perception target, memory target, policy target) rather than in *channel language* (α-channel, β-channel, plasticity-gating-channel). Each lens then applies its own naming as an *interpretive overlay* the Discussion section deploys after the data lands. The substrate-level claim is lens-independent.

## 4. What this redirects the project away from

The continual-learning win is one behavioural readout of an LC-NA-analog phasic-burst-at-regime-change signature, not evidence that NMN helps continual learning *per se*. The closest published precedent is **Lee et al. 2024 §3.1 + Figure 1**, which proposes ACh and NA as modulators of RL hyperparameters in a non-stationary bandit with hand-coded functional forms — and which **explicitly drops the 5-HT/γ branch in §6** because no convergent normative theory exists. v5 follows Lee 2024 §6's precedent for the *non-targeted* systems (ACh, DA, 5-HT): named honestly as out-of-paper-scope future work, with the project committing to NA / LC as the targeted exemplar. The project's contribution under v5 is **Lee 2024's two-channel framework re-formulated under the user's refined "target one, acknowledge many, defer the rest" constraint: a multi-site FiLM substrate computationally instantiating the LC-NA "one signal, many effects via target-specific readouts" principle**, with the mapping emerging from optimisation rather than being hand-coded, in sequential RL (PPO) rather than bandits, with the FiLM ⊂ Hypernet ⊂ point-mass-BHN-limit lineage made explicit (§3.0) so the substrate's architectural ancestry is no longer implicit.

### 5.1 Headline falsifier — combined γ/β-freeze + |δ|-tracking with three outcome scenarios (policy target, site C)

**Plain-English summary.** At the policy target (site C, where the modulator's signals control how sharply the agent commits to an action), we run a four-arm experiment: full modulator, frozen multiplicative arm, frozen additive arm, both frozen. We then ask which arm carried the load, and whether the surviving arm tracks how surprised the value-head is on each step. Three different outcomes correspond to three different stories about what the modulator is actually doing.

**The four arms.**

1. *Baseline* — full FiLM, both γ and β free.
2. *γ-clamp at C* — $\gamma^{(C)} = 1$ throughout; only $\beta^{(C)}$ moves.
3. *β-clamp at C* — $\beta^{(C)} = 0$ throughout; only $\gamma^{(C)}$ moves.
4. *Full clamp at C* — both arms frozen.

**Per-arm measures.** (i) Survival-step recovery half-life after a stage switch; (ii) actor-entropy peri-switch spike amplitude (`train/entropy_loss`); (iii) Pearson correlation of the surviving FiLM-arm output with $|\delta|$ over the recovery window (`r_filmarm_vs_abs_delta_per_clamp`); (iv) **sensitivity $d'$ and decision criterion under a forced-choice probe trial** (added in v3 from the Bayesian-brain lens; requires the forced-choice probe described in §5.8 to be feasible; if not, demote to future work).

**The three outcomes.**

- **Outcome U (Unification holds at C).** γ-clamp is more damaging than β-clamp on survival-step recovery AND entropy spike; the surviving β-arm correlates with $|\delta|$. Reading under the substrate-level claim: the multiplicative arm at the policy target is producing the load-bearing effect, with the additive arm carrying the TD-error-driven shift. **The basket-of-effects framing then refines Outcome U further**: under the Bayesian-brain lens (Friston 2010; Pezzulo et al. 2015), if γ-clamp degrades $d'$ specifically (not just criterion), the multiplicative arm at C is producing a **policy-precision-like effect** ($\Pi_\pi$ in predictive-coding terms) — a richer reading consistent with one signal modulating multiple precisions at multiple targets. If γ-clamp leaves $d'$ untouched and only shifts criterion, the Doya-channel lens (NA-β as inverse temperature alone) is the parsimonious reading. Both refinements live within "Unification holds" — they distinguish a Bayesian-brain reading from a bare Doya-NA reading inside the same falsifier arm, demonstrating *which interpretive lens* fits the data without changing the substrate-level claim.
- **Outcome V (Vecoven context-conditioning, no Doya channel).** Both clamps are roughly equally damaging AND neither surviving arm tracks $|\delta|$. Reading: the modulator is a *task-identity detector* (Vecoven 2020 §4.4–4.5 freeze-$z$ precedent), not a target-flexible gain modulator producing baskets of effects. The substrate-level claim *partially* survives — the modulator does dissociate behaviour by context — but the basket-of-effects interpretation does not, because the modulator's effect at C is not a temperature/precision/action-prior basket; it is a context-conditional switch. The project's contribution shrinks to "FiLM as task-identity detection on PPO".
- **Outcome T (Tsuda hypertube dynamics dominate).** β-clamp is more damaging than γ-clamp. Reading: the additive arm at C is producing activity-space displacements (Tsuda 2021 Fig. 3 hypertubes — analogously at the level of geometric signature; Tsuda's *mechanism* is multiplicative-on-weights). Gain control is the right story; the basket-of-effects framing has to be walked back at C because one specific arm (additive) is doing the load-bearing work.

**Why this is the headline.** The synthesis ([neuromodulatory_algorithms_predictions_synthesis.md §3.6 / §5.1](../references/neuromodulatory_algorithms/neuromodulatory_algorithms_predictions_synthesis.md)) flags as load-bearing that **no paper in the 18-paper corpus runs the symmetric two-direction unification test on the same agent on the same trials**. v5 inherits this as the closest the project can get to that test on a forward-pass FiLM substrate — Outcome U is the publishable headline, Outcomes V and T are honest publishable refutations. The d′-vs-criterion sharpening inside Outcome U is an internal lens-discrimination that strengthens whichever reading the data supports.

**A fifth no-go outcome.** A four-way tie (all four clamps perform the same) refutes the FiLM machinery as load-bearing at all and is a pre-flight check on the modulator's training signal.

### 5.2 Perception-target predictions (site A, encoder pre-fusion)

**Plain-English summary.** The encoder is the place where the agent's incoming sensory channels — exteroception, nociception, interoception, threat-signal coupling, etc. — get embedded into a representation the policy can act on. Under the substrate-level claim, the modulator's effect at the perception target produces a *basket* of behavioural-knob-like effects on that representation: a learning-rate-like effect (per-feature gain on representation contributes to downstream gradient flow), a sensory-precision-like effect (channel-selective trust in observation), a divisive-normalisation-like effect (high gain on one feature reduces relative weight of others), and an expected-uncertainty-like effect (sensitivity to noisy-observation regime change). All four are *simultaneously plausible* downstream effects of the same modulator signal acting through the perception-target readout matrix; v5 does not commit to any one of them as *the* effect.

- *Cellular mechanism (substrate-level).* Mixed multiplicative and additive arm at A (Ferguson & Cardin 2020 Box 1 panels b and c). Perceptual gain on evidence is canonically a mixture (Shine 2021 Fig. 1). The neuromodulation lens predicts a divisive-normalisation signature: high $\gamma_{A,i}$ on one feature reduces relative weight of competing features in the downstream policy decision (`eval/gamma_A_divisive_index` < 1; from neuromod P7).
- *Effect-flavours in the basket at A (the four lenses' readings).*
  - Doya-channel lens: ACh-as-learning-rate α (Doya 2002 §3.4; Lee 2024 §3.1).
  - Precision-coding lens: sensory precision $\Pi_s$ (Adams, Shipp & Friston 2013; Shine 2021 Box 3).
  - Hypernet / BHN lens: per-target readout at the perception observation point producing channel-selective gain, with the dissociation living in the readout matrix.
  - Target-flexibility lens: receptor-density-analog readout at the perception target produces a *basket* whose specific composition depends on the readout's learned weights — multiple effect-flavours co-arise rather than one channel emerging.
- *Pain construct.* **Channel-selective hypervigilance pattern** (pain-modeling §C re-stated dissociation 1, behaviourally defined: the post-injury gain pattern on threat-relevant input modalities is non-uniformly elevated, by a margin of $\Delta\gamma_{\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{N}} \ge 0.2$ across $\ge 5$ seeds, *regardless of which injection site carries the elevation*). Without this dissociation the modulator is doing volume control at the perception target, not hypervigilance.
- *Project-testbed measures.* (i) `delta_survival_clamp_A` largest on noisy-observation tasks; (ii) per-modality $\gamma_A$ statistics in event-locked windows across the 9 input modalities; (iii) site-A response decoupling under heteroscedastic noise — large under high-obs-noise / clean-reward, small under clean-obs / stochastic-reward (Bayesian-brain P1); (iv) `eval/gamma_A_divisive_index` < 1 for divisive-normalisation signature (neuromod P7); (v) cross-modality gain non-uniformity for the §5.7 re-stated dissociation 1.
- *Falsification signature (substrate-level).* If site-A clamp damages all task axes equally, or if $\gamma_A$ rises uniformly across modalities post-injury, the basket-of-effects framing collapses at the perception target: the modulator is doing volume control at A rather than producing target-specific effects. The substrate-level claim retreats to "modulator does basket-of-effects work at *some* targets but not the perception target".

### 5.3 Policy-target predictions (site C, policy logits)

**Plain-English summary.** The policy target is where the modulator's signals control how sharply the agent commits to one action over another. Under the substrate-level claim, the basket of effects at C includes *temperature-like* effects (NA inverse temperature; Doya), *action-prior-shift* effects (additive β at C as a rheobase-on-policy analogue), *attentional-bias* effects (Aston-Jones tonic-vs-phasic mode), *policy-precision-like* effects (active-inference $\gamma_G$ on expected free energy; Friston-Pezzulo), and *exploration-bonus-like* effects (Aston-Jones exploitation / exploration arbitration). Five effect-flavours plausibly co-arise at the policy target under one modulator signal acting through the policy-target readout.

- *Cellular mechanism (substrate-level).* Multiplicative arm dominant ($\gamma_C$) — Ferguson & Cardin 2020 Box 1b. Scaling actor logits is, on the multiplicative arm, a slope change on the softmax (neuromod P3 — multiplicative is the only operation that produces a temperature movement). The additive arm $\beta_C$ shifts the decision boundary between actions and is the rheobase-on-policy analogue. Per math-reviewer audit minor revision (5) on the concept memo §5.4 Row EE-1: $\gamma_C \equiv 1/T$ only on the multiplicative arm; $\beta_C$ has a separate behavioural interpretation as an action-prior shift, not as part of the inverse-temperature mapping.
- *Effect-flavours in the basket at C (the four lenses' readings).*
  - Doya-channel lens: NA-as-inverse-temperature β (Doya 2002 §3.3; Lee 2024 §3.4 Eq. 4 — $\beta^{-1}(s) = 1/\langle E(s,\hat a)\rangle_{\hat a}$).
  - Precision-coding lens: policy precision $\Pi_\pi$ — how sharply to commit to one policy. Under active inference (Friston, Rigoli et al. 2015), this is also the inverse-temperature on expected free energy $G(\pi)$.
  - Hypernet / BHN lens: per-target readout at the policy observation point producing dissociable behavioural-fingerprint effects.
  - Target-flexibility lens: LC-NA-analog phasic burst acting on the policy target through the policy readout, with the basket's specific composition determined by the readout's learned weights.
- *Pain construct.* **Fear-avoidance as sustained tonic policy-entropy reduction** (Vlaeyen & Linton 2000; L'Haridon & Cañamero 2023 cortisol-modulated robot pain). Tonic site-C output is more negative (lower temperature, lower entropy) in spatial regions proximal to past injury events, *outlasting the injury input*. Pain-modeling memo's §C re-stated dissociation 3 is the cross-domain coupling condition that has to hold simultaneously.
- *Project-testbed measures.* (i) `delta_survival_clamp_C` largest on regime-change recovery; (ii) `mod_h_to_beta_eff_R2` at C ≥ 0.5 while same scalar at A, B < 0.3; (iii) per-tile visitation entropy in near-injury vs far-from-injury regions, post-recovery; (iv) regression of $\gamma^{(C)}$ on value-prediction-error magnitude in per-site partial regression (Bayesian-brain P3).
- *Falsification signature (substrate-level).* If $\gamma_C$ effective rank is high (> 5) and the per-site clamp dominates all task axes, the policy-target basket is overparameterised and the single-channel-LC-NA-with-passengers reading wins. Under the basket framing this means the policy target is producing *fewer* dissociable effects than the substrate-level claim predicts, not zero — the modulator's effect at C may still produce multiple flavours, but the readout matrix is too low-rank to dissociate them.

### 5.4 Memory-target / plasticity-gating predictions (site B, GRU update gate)

**Plain-English summary.** The memory target is where the modulator's signals control how strongly the agent's recurrent hidden state retains past information vs. integrates new evidence. Under the substrate-level claim, the basket at B includes *plasticity-gating* (per-unit memory persistence via $\tau_h$), *state-transition-precision-like* effects (Bayesian-brain), *memory-consolidation-like* effects (NA / Sara 2009; ACh / Hasselmo), and *prior-precision-elevation-like* effects (the pain-modeling §C re-stated dissociation 3 condition for tonic threat-prior elevation).

**Inheritance note (per math-reviewer flag (d) at v4).** v5 keeps v4's correction: site B is *plasticity gating*, not γ_Bellman. The GRU update gate $z_t \in (0,1)^H$ produces per-unit hidden-state retention per inner-network-timestep; Bellman γ is a global scalar inside a temporal sum. The two can correlate empirically but are not the same quantity. The §5.9 boundary preserves this.

- *Cellular mechanism (substrate-level).* Additive arm dominant ($\beta_B$) — Ferguson & Cardin 2020 Box 1c (rheobase shift). The GRU update gate $z_t = \sigma(W_z [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_z + \beta^{(B)})$ — additive $\beta^{(B)}$ shifts the "stay vs update" decision boundary without changing sensitivity to input.
- *Effect-flavours in the basket at B (the four lenses' readings).*
  - Doya-channel lens: plasticity-gating, *not* γ_Bellman. Costacurta 2024 Prop. 1 (LSTM-equivalence of low-rank weight scaling) supplies the structural precedent.
  - Precision-coding lens: state-transition precision $\Pi_z$ — how much to trust the prior over hidden states relative to incoming evidence (Adams, Shipp & Friston 2013).
  - Hypernet / BHN lens: per-target readout at the memory observation point producing per-unit retention dissociation.
  - Target-flexibility lens: NA-analog or ACh-analog signal acting on memory consolidation through the memory readout — biology routinely produces multi-effect work at memory circuits via multiple modulators converging.
- *Pain construct.* **Tonic prior-precision elevation** (pain-modeling memo's symposium §C; Büchel et al. 2014; Wiech 2016; Tabor & Burr 2019). Post-recovery tonic $\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}}$ stays elevated relative to pre-injury baseline without sustained actor-entropy change. This is the Bayesian-brain reading of chronic-pain-like hypervigilance: the threat prior's precision grows, not the policy temperature.
- *Project-testbed measures.* (i) `delta_survival_clamp_B` largest on memory-required predator-avoidance and long-dormancy R2/R3 return tasks (neuromod P8 + RL/BDL P-3); (ii) tonic-state ratio post-recovery / pre-injury baseline; (iii) site-B response loading disproportionately on state-prediction-error magnitude in the per-site partial regression (Bayesian-brain P3); (iv) **recurrent-state autocorrelation time constant $\tau_h$** under modulated vs clamped site B (the corrected operationalisation per math-reviewer required revision 2 — the auto-correlation of the GRU hidden state, NOT a γ_Bellman estimate).
- *Falsification signature (substrate-level).* If site-B clamp's deficit is small or distributes uniformly across all subtests, the memory-target basket framing collapses — the site is a passenger head with no distinct effect on memory dynamics.

**Joint prediction across the three targets — the target-specificity test.** The neuromodulation memo's P8 sharpens the per-target predictions into a *qualitative-profile* test: the three targets should produce *qualitatively dissociable* deficits, not just *quantitatively different* magnitudes. Operationally, the 3 × 3 deficit matrix `eval/per_site_freeze_per_subtest_drop` (A-freeze, B-freeze, C-freeze × noisy-obs, long-dormancy R2/R3 return, regime-change recovery) should show **diagonal dominance** — each target's freeze produces its largest drop on its predicted subtest. Under the substrate-level claim, this diagonal dominance is the **receptor-density-as-readout signature** (one signal, three target-specific readouts producing target-specific effects), *not* a channel-dissociation signature. The measurement is the same as v4 §5.4; the interpretation is biologically richer.

### 5.5 Phasic-vs-tonic predictions (conditional on T/P split) — multifunctionality basket-effect test

**Plain-English summary.** Biology dissociates two timescales of neuromodulator action: brief bursts (phasic, milliseconds–seconds) and sustained shifts (tonic, seconds–minutes). The corpus repeatedly invokes this dissociation, but no published forward-pass FiLM-style machine has two recurrent timescales with separable readouts. The project's Phase 0 T/P split is the architectural change that makes the dissociation testable. v5 updates the phasic-tonic predictions to **basket-effect tests by timescale**: each timescale should drive a *basket* of effects simultaneously across the three targets, *not* a single named effect. This is the symposium's load-bearing test of multifunctionality at the timescale level, per neuromodulation memo's symposium idea D.3.

- **§5.5.1 — Phasic-vs-tonic timescale separation (RL / Bayesian-DL P-7).** Post-T/P-split, the phasic state $P$ should track $|\delta|$ with an autocorrelation time-constant $\tau_P \in [1, 5]$ episodes; the tonic state $T$ should track cumulative cost or running value-baseline error with $\tau_T \in [50, 200]$ episodes. The separation factor $\tau_T / \tau_P \ge 10$ is the empirical signature of a successful split. WandB readouts: `mod_h_P_autocorr_tau`, `mod_h_T_autocorr_tau`. **Falsification**: $\tau_T / \tau_P < 3$ means the two recurrent compartments collapsed under optimisation pressure — the multi-effect-at-multiple-targets claim remains non-identifiable on this testbed and v5's headline must be conditional on its success.
- **§5.5.2 — Differential phasic / tonic basket-effect test (NEW under v5; neuromod symposium D.3).** Under one-to-many, each timescale should drive a *basket* of effects simultaneously, not one effect per timescale. Specifically:
  - The **phasic state $P$** should drive, *all at once on the same regime-change event*: a temperature-like spike at the policy target (site C γ deviation); a sensory-precision-like spike at the perception target (site A γ deviation on threat-relevant modalities); a plasticity-gating burst at the memory target (site B β deviation modulating $\tau_h$); and a $|\delta|$-tracking signal at C. Operationally: phasic state's partial correlations with $\{|\delta|, \text{entropy spike at C}, \gamma_A \text{ spike on threat-relevant modalities}, \tau_h \text{ burst at B}\}$ should *all* be positive simultaneously over a regime-change window.
  - The **tonic state $T$** should drive, *all at once over sustained aversive exposure*: prior-precision elevation at A (sustained $\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}}$ elevation on threat-relevant modalities); fear-avoidance-like entropy reduction at C (lower tonic policy entropy in near-injury regions); long-term plasticity shift at B (sustained $\tau_h$ elevation); and a slow integral of cost / aversive-signal exposure. Operationally: tonic state's partial correlations with $\{\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}} \text{ tonic level}, \text{near-injury entropy}, \tau_h \text{ tonic}, \text{cumulative cost}\}$ should *all* be positive simultaneously over a post-recovery window.
  
  This is a **basket-effect test by timescale**. Two failure modes refute the multifunctionality reading: (i) P and T converge (effective rank 1 across the two-state representation); (ii) P drives only one effect-flavour rather than a basket (e.g., P tracks $|\delta|$ but does not drive simultaneous spikes at A and B) — the substrate-level basket-of-effects framing partially fails, with the timescale separation producing single-effect-per-timescale rather than basket-per-timescale.
- **§5.5.3 — Tonic content under sustained aversive exposure (pain-modeling P-Pain-2 + neuromod P2 cross-link).** The tonic state should enter a high near-absorbing state after sustained aversive exposure and not relax even when the aversive regime passes. This is the substrate-level signature of hypervigilance / chronic-pain analogues. **Construct-validity caveat (from pain-modeling P-Pain-2)**: long $\tau_T$ alone is not a prior-precision claim. The dissociating feature requires a **cross-context transfer probe** — the modulator's persistent state generalises to a novel post-recovery context, not just to the same one. Without that probe (§5.8), this is "recurrent inertia mis-labelled as chronicity", not chronic-pain analog.

### 5.6 Architecture-discrimination predictions

These predictions discriminate the project's forward-pass FiLM substrate from architectural alternatives (hypernets, conditional batch norm, Rodriguez-Garcia 2026 gradient-level gain). They are the predictions that ride on substrate-specific signatures rather than on behaviour. **v5 inherits §5.6 unchanged from v4** — the architecture-discrimination measurements are interpretable under all four lenses identically.

- **§5.6.1 — Effective rank of γ at site C (RL/BDL P-2; neuromod P5).** SVD on $\gamma^{(C)}_t$ stacked across a regime-change window; effective rank $r_{\text{eff}}(\gamma^{(C)}) = (\sum_i \sigma_i)^2 / \sum_i \sigma_i^2$. **Prediction under the substrate-level basket-of-effects reading**: $r_{\text{eff}}^{(C)} > 1$ AND principal directions at A, B, C are orthogonal (cross-site principal angle large). WandB readouts: `gamma_C_effective_rank`, `gamma_AB_principal_angle`. **Vecoven 2020 Fig. 7** is the published precedent for this measurement; Costacurta 2024 Fig. 3F is the closest dissociation-by-ablation precedent. Falsification: $r_{\text{eff}}^{(C)} \ge 5$ → FiLM at C is doing high-dimensional gain control beyond what any lens predicts, and v5's claim should be "multi-dimensional gain controller", not "target-specific basket-of-effects modulator".
- **§5.6.2 — Cross-site cross-correlation lag structure (neuromod P6).** Pearson cross-correlation between $\gamma^{(A)}(t), \gamma^{(B)}(t), \gamma^{(C)}(t)$ at lags $\in \{-5, -1, 0, 1, 5\}$ episodes. **Pre-T/P prediction**: all zero-lag cross-correlations > 0.9 — confirms single-channel-with-passengers. **Post-T/P prediction**: zero-lag cross-correlations drop to < 0.7 and lag-structure emerges with $\gamma^{(B)}$ trailing $\gamma^{(C)}$ by several episodes. Post-T/P zero-lag cross-correlations remaining > 0.9 refute the dissociation.
- **§5.6.3 — Hessian-eigenvalue probe discriminating forward-pass FiLM from Rodriguez-Garcia 2026 (RL/BDL P-6; corrected per math-reviewer required revision 1).** Around each regime change, compute top-$k$ eigenvalues of the PPO loss Hessian over a small batch. **Corrected discriminator direction** (per math-reviewer audit of concept memo §6.3 signature 2): under Rodriguez-Garcia 2026, the *raw-weight Hessian* $H_W$ is **amplified by $g^2$** (not flattened); the flattening lives at the effective-parameter-space Hessian $H_{W_{\text{eff}}} = H_W / g^2$. The discriminating measurement is therefore the **shift direction of $H_W$** — amplification under Rodriguez-Garcia, no change under forward-pass FiLM. **Prediction**: FiLM agent and unmodulated baseline both show *similar* top-eigenvalue trajectories peri-switch at $H_W$ (no amplification); a Rodriguez-Garcia-style NGM-SGD agent if implemented as a comparator should show $H_W$ amplification proportional to $g^2$. **Falsification (Failure mode 1)**: FiLM agent shows $H_W$ amplification — the orthogonality claim in §3 is wrong; forward-pass FiLM is leaking into optimiser dynamics (publishable finding in its own right). **Failure mode 2**: unmodulated baseline shows amplification (PPO's clipping providing it via a different route) — the discriminating measure is not load-bearing.
- **§5.6.4 — Per-feature gain variance discriminating FiLM from hypernet (RL/BDL P-9).** Compute $\text{Var}_t[\gamma_i^{(C)}]$ across a regime-change window for each feature index $i$; report the distribution of per-feature variances. **Prediction**: FiLM shows high p90/p10 ratio (a few features carry the change); a hypothetical hypernet ablation should show lower ratio. Falsification: uniform per-feature variance → FiLM is collapsing to a Rodriguez-Garcia-style scalar gain and the high-dimensional FiLM substrate is wasted.
- **§5.6.5 — Cross-seed channel-attribution consistency (RL/BDL P-10).** Train two identical FiLM agents with different random seeds *and* different random initialisations of the three read-out matrices. Check whether the per-site clamp-deficit profile is consistent (Spearman correlation across seed pairs ≥ 0.5). High consistency: the task breaks the symmetry and the target-specificity claim is recoverable. Low consistency (< 0.3): channel attribution is seed-dependent. This is the *identifiability prerequisite test* the single-`mod_h` design imposes. **Note under v5's behaviourally-defined framing**: the re-stated pain gate (§5.7) is seed-invariant — it measures behavioural-effect patterns, not site assignments — so cross-seed inconsistency at this probe does *not* refute the §5.7 gate, only the strong-form lens-specific channel-attribution claim.

### 5.7 Pain-construct gate-keeping (behaviourally-defined)

This subsection is the most important contribution of the pain-modeling lens to v3 / v4 / v5. **v5 rewrites the four-dissociation gate in behavioural-effect language, per the pain-modeling memo's symposium §C.** The original gate was channel-coded ("γ-channel-selective at site A; site-B memory shift; site-C policy shift"); under the user's no-one-to-one constraint, that gate cannot survive — it implicitly commits to which Doya channel implements which clinical operationalisation of hypervigilance. The re-stated gate is *behaviourally-defined*: dissociations on the pattern of behavioural effects the agent produces, without commitment to which site or which channel implements them. This is *more biologically defensible* than the channel-coded form, because real neuromodulators *do* jointly affect multiple functions — joint within-trial coupling is the expected signature of any biologically-realistic modulator, not a one-to-one channel commitment.

**The four-dissociation gate, re-stated in behavioural-effect language.**

1. **Channel-selective gain shifts on threat-relevant inputs, regardless of injection site.** The post-injury gain pattern on the encoder's input modalities is non-uniform, with threat-relevant modalities receiving systematically larger upward shifts than threat-irrelevant ones, by a margin of $\Delta\gamma_{\mathcal{T} \setminus \mathcal{N}} \ge 0.2$ across $\ge 5$ seeds. The dissociation is on the *behavioural-readout* gain pattern, not on which Doya channel implements it. Whether the read-out is purely at site A, partly via site C feeding back into the encoder via the recurrent loop, or a mixture, is unspecified. Consistent with the clinical operationalisation of hypervigilance at the attentional level (Eccleston & Crombez 1999; Crombez et al. 2005; Wiech 2016).
2. **Time-locked to interoceptive injury inference, not to threat input.** The gain pattern in dissociation 1 lags injury-channel onset by $\le \min(5, 2\tau_{\text{mod}})$ steps and is insensitive to predator-presence change without injury. Dissociates pain-computation from task-relevance-learning at the temporal level. Site-agnostic.
3. **Joint perceptual–mnemonic–policy reweighting through a common modulator state.** Within-trial cross-domain coupling $r \ge 0.3$ at lag $\le 5$ between gain shifts at the perception, memory, and policy targets on the same trial, same seed. Under no-one-to-one this remains testable but is re-interpreted: the dissociation does not require that these three shifts correspond to three separable neuromodulators — it requires only that *behavioural effects at perception, memory, and policy are co-modulated within a trial* — riding on a *common* slow state, whatever Doya channels that state instantiates. This is the structural core of Vlaeyen & Linton 2000's fear-avoidance model and Crombez et al. 2005's "concern-dependent" framing.
4. **Dissociation from a slow-input C1 EMA baseline.** The modulated agent's signature must differ from a trainable exponential-moving-average-of-nociception baseline wired through the same A/B/C heads ([pain_vs_nociception_construct.md §3](../concepts/pain_vs_nociception_construct.md)). The contrast metric becomes: *the modulated agent produces a non-uniform across-channel gain pattern (dissociation 1) that the C1 EMA baseline cannot reproduce because the EMA has only one scalar to assign*. Channel-agnostic on the architectural side.

**Behavioural signatures that do NOT count (P-Pain-5 negative predictions, inherited from v4 §5.7 unchanged).**

- *(P-Pain-5a)* Survival increase under modulator on regime change is consistent with the substrate-level claim, with continual-learning consolidation, with LC-NA-as-context-detector, and with simple modulator-state inertia. Not evidence on any of the four dissociations above.
- *(P-Pain-5b)* Modulator burst at injury onset is the canonical LC-NA unexpected-uncertainty signature (Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005; Wainstein 2025). Not pain-content on its own.
- *(P-Pain-5c)* Slow recovery of survival after injury is consistent with any slow-state architecture. Requires cross-context transfer probe (§5.8).
- *(P-Pain-5d)* Increased aversion to nociceptive input is what reward shaping on `extero_noc` produces. Pain content requires dissociability from the input.
- *(P-Pain-5e)* Continual-learning resistance is *one readout* of state-dependent hyperparameter modulation, not pain content.

**Construct-validity verdict.** If all four re-stated dissociations hold, "pain-like hypervigilance signature" is defensible at a *PAIN* or *Nature Mental Health* reviewer. If any one fails, v5 retreats to the formulation "interoceptive neuromodulation as a substrate for pain-like behaviour" ([pain_vs_nociception_construct.md §4.4](../concepts/pain_vs_nociception_construct.md)). The R2 empirical anchor is — by itself — not evidence on any of the four dissociations.

v5 inherits v4 §5.8 unchanged. The current testbed + T/P split tests directly: §5.1 four-arm clamp at C (except the $d'$/criterion refinement which needs a forced-choice probe); §5.2 / §5.3 / §5.4 per-target clamp deficits and qualitative profiles; §5.5.1 and §5.5.2 phasic / tonic separation and basket-effect tests; §5.6 architecture-discrimination predictions; three of four re-stated §5.7 dissociations (the fourth requires the C1 EMA baseline configuration).

### 5.9 What the substrate explicitly does NOT carry

**Plain-English summary.** v5's substrate-level claim is anchored on *one targeted system* (LC-NA) and its *fan-out into target-specific baskets of effects at three injection sites*. This subsection draws the honesty boundary at the predictions level — four classes of modulation that look like they should belong to this substrate but do not.

**No-map 1 — γ_Bellman / 5-HT (Doya 2002 §3.2).** Inherited from v4 §5.9. The 5-HT-as-discount mapping has no implementation in either the FiLM corpus or the neuromodulation corpus. Lee 2024 §6 explicitly dropped 5-HT. The clean architectural home for γ_Bellman is the TD-target computation at the loss / learning-update layer, not the forward pass. A FiLM substrate cannot carry this channel without architectural extension into a hybrid system (forward-pass FiLM + a learned-discount head feeding the TD target, [investigation memo §3.2 Candidate d](../concepts/film_hypernet_bnn_lineage_and_critic_modulation.md)). v5 follows Lee 2024.

**No-map 2 — gradient-level gain (Rodriguez-Garcia 2026).** Inherited from v4 §5.9. Rodriguez-Garcia 2026's neuromodulator-gated SGD (NGM-SGD) modulates the *gradient* via $W_{ij}(t) = g_i(t) w_{ij}(t)$, reparameterising the learning rate as $\lambda \to \lambda / g^2$. This is complementary to the forward-pass FiLM substrate — both invoke noradrenergic gain at orthogonal points (forward activation vs gradient). The §5.6.3 Hessian-eigenvalue probe is the project-side measurement that would discriminate the two substrates.

**No-map 3 — internal-RNN gain (Wainstein 2025).** Inherited from v4 §5.9. Wainstein's $g$ is an experimenter-varied dial on a post-hoc trained-RNN analysis, not a side-signal-conditioned forward-pass operator.

**No-map 4 — full coverage of ACh + DA + 5-HT systems (NEW under v5's refined constraint).** The user's refined constraint is explicit: *target one system, acknowledge one-to-many at that system, defer full coverage to future work*. v5 targets LC-NA. The ACh, DA, and 5-HT systems each produce multiple downstream effects on the substrate — ACh's learning-rate / expected-uncertainty / divisive-normalisation triad (Yu & Dayan 2005; Sarter & Lustig; Hasselmo); DA's RPE / vigor / policy-precision triad (Schultz / Niv / Friston); 5-HT's discount / aversive-opponent / reflective-inhibition triad (Doya / Daw / Dayan & Huys) — but the project does *not* claim to cover them in this paper. The neuromodulation memo's symposium contribution is explicit: full coverage of all neuromodulator-function mappings by one-to-one is *mathematically impossible* because biological function is overlapped and distributed; the project's task is to demonstrate the principle on one targeted system, with the other systems named as future work. This boundary is the structural commitment v5 makes that v4 did not.

### 5.10 Future work flagged but not committed in v5

Three items are flagged here so that v5's predictions section is closed on the current substrate, but the project has a record of where a v6 (or a parallel sub-project) could go:

1. **Active-inference / EFE temperature reading at site C** (Bayesian-brain P2 + P7). The Friston / Pezzulo / Parr active-inference literature is out-of-corpus; the EFE-temperature reading is mathematically rich but its discriminating test (information-seeking under high observation noise) requires the information-action probe (§5.8). v5 keeps the substrate-level claim as load-bearing at C and flags the EFE reading as a future-work alternative. The current $d'$/criterion refinement (§5.1 + P4) is the down-payment.
2. **γ_Bellman / 5-HT as a hybrid extension** ([investigation memo §3.5 Option B](../concepts/film_hypernet_bnn_lineage_and_critic_modulation.md)). v5 adopts Option A — drop the 5-HT/γ channel from substrate claims. A v6 could adopt Option B — add a learned-discount network $\hat\gamma(c)$ as a fourth modulator arm consumed in the TD target.
3. **Multifunctionality index $M$ as a candidate readout** (NEW; neuromod symposium D.1). Define a scalar $M$ — the multifunctionality index — that counts how many distinct algorithmic-level behavioural effect-flavours `mod_h` produces simultaneously across the three targets. For each of $k$ candidate readings (Doya-α, Doya-β, ACh-precision, NA-tonic, NA-phasic, plasticity-gating, divisive-normalisation, action-prior-shift, exploration-bonus, etc.), compute the partial correlation between `mod_h` and the reading-specific empirical signature, controlling for the other $k-1$. $M$ = number of partial correlations above a pre-registered threshold. $M \ge 4$ across three targets driven by one `mod_h` is the strongest biological-plausibility claim *without* committing to which four effects are the four. Surfaced here as a candidate measurement that v5 does *not* commit to but a v6 could adopt; the measurement re-uses already-planned experiments.

### 5.11 Multifunctionality readouts (NEW)

This subsection surfaces three readouts on already-planned experiments that operationalise the substrate-level multifunctionality claim. Each is a *new readout on existing v4 §5 measurements*, not a new experiment.

**Readout 1 — Multifunctionality index $M$ (neuromod D.1).** Count the number of partial correlations between `mod_h` and $k$ candidate algorithmic-readout signatures (Doya-α, Doya-β, ACh-precision, NA-tonic, NA-phasic, plasticity-gating, divisive-normalisation, action-prior-shift, exploration-bonus, expected-uncertainty, policy-precision, sensory-precision, state-transition-precision, …) exceeding a pre-registered threshold. Each partial correlation controls for the other $k-1$. Interpretation: $M = 1$ → single-channel-with-passengers (v4's worst-case reading); $M \ge 4$ across three targets driven by one `mod_h` → **the one-to-many regime under v5's refined constraint**; the modulator produces a basket of effects via target-specific readouts, with the basket's size measured as $M$. The neuromodulation memo's D.1 idea is the source. *Note*: this readout is surfaced here but **not committed in v5's experimental scope** — it is a re-analysis recipe on the v4 §5 program's already-planned measurements, and the user's pace flag argues for landing v4 §5 first and deferring $M$ to a follow-up analysis.

**Readout 2 — Target-specificity ratio $T$ (neuromod D.2).** Compute, on the 3 × 3 deficit matrix `eval/per_site_freeze_per_subtest_drop` (already in v4 §5.4), the diagonal-dominance ratio $T = (\text{diagonal sum})/(\text{off-diagonal sum})$, values > 1 meaning each target is preferentially responsible for its predicted subtest. v4 already plans this matrix; v5's reframing under the receptor-density-as-readout lens is to **interpret $T$ as the receptor-density-fan-out signature** rather than as the channel-dissociation signature. Under one-to-many, there are no channels to dissociate; what dissociates is the *target-specific effect of one signal* through three different readout matrices. Same measurement; biologically richer interpretation. The architectural claim becomes "we built a learned receptor-density-fan-out", not "we built a multi-channel modulator".

**Readout 3 — Basket-effect dissociation by timescale (neuromod D.3; §5.5.2 above).** Each timescale (phasic $P$, tonic $T$) should drive a basket of effects simultaneously across the three targets, not one effect per timescale. Operationalised in §5.5.2 as partial-correlation tests against four effect-flavours per timescale; the readout is the *count* of significant simultaneous-positive partial correlations per timescale. Phasic basket size $\ge 3$ and tonic basket size $\ge 3$ on the same `mod_h` is the strongest multifunctionality test the project's testbed + T/P split supports. This readout *is* in v5's experimental scope (it is the test §5.5.2 names directly), unlike $M$ which is positioned as future-work re-analysis.

## 11. Path options for the experimental phase

This section is **path-neutral**. v5's body (sections 1–10) is consistent with each of the four paths the PI surfaced via the [20260516 impactful-vs-reasonable call](../../pi/calls/2026-05-16_impact_vs_reasonable_call.md). The choice among them is the **user's next decision** — surfaced by the PI via `AskUserQuestion` — and fixes the experimental scope, the venue family, and the submission timeline. v5 does not commit; v5 lays the four paths out so the user can pick after reading v5.

The PI's recommended ranking (under identity-level reading of "impactful"): Path A > Path E > Path B > Path C > Path D. Under field-level reading: Path B > Path A > Path D > Path E > Path C. The user owns the choice between the two readings.

### Path A — NA-targeted multi-effect demonstration (PI's top recommendation)

**Headline (plain English).** A small modulator network, trained end-to-end on an interoceptive RL agent, suggests the *possibility* that a single noradrenaline-like ascending signal can produce multiple behavioural-knob-like effects (faster recovery on returning to a known stage; sign-consistent temperature swings at stage boundaries; dissociable per-target clamps), via target-specific readout matrices that are the in-silico analogue of receptor density. We do not claim to recover Doya's four channels; we claim one demonstration of the multifunctional-gain principle, with LC-NA as the named biological exemplar.

**Impactful-vs-reasonable position.** Reasonable end of the spectrum. Strong on the biological-substrate side (LC-NA is the canonical regime-change burst system; the R2 anchor's sign-consistent swings at stage boundaries fit Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005's phasic pattern); softer on the architectural-novelty side (Row EE-6 stays as future work).

**Venue family.** *Neural Computation* / *PLOS Computational Biology* / *eLife computational neuroscience* / CCN / COSYNE. NeurIPS bio-inspired track plausible but not natural home.

**Compute / planning cost.** Zero new compute beyond v5's substrate. The v5 §5 experimental program (per-target clamps, 3 × 3 dissociation matrix, T/P split as Phase 0 prerequisite, three-seed replication of R2) is the program. No new architectural extensions.

**Submission timeline.** Earliest of the four paths in the postdoc-recommended set. 2–4 months wall-clock if launches start within ~2 weeks of v5 landing.

**Risk profile.** Lowest. Backward-compatible with everything the project has built. The professor convergence is unanimous that this framing is defensible.

**Map to v5's body.** Path A *is* v5's substrate-level claim with v5 §5 as the experimental program. No additional scope.

### Path B — Architectural-lead (Approximate Bayesian Hypernetwork)

**Headline (plain English).** First introduction of an approximate Bayesian Hypernetwork (BHN — a network that produces a *distribution* over another network's weights as a function of input context) for an RL policy, with heteroscedastic likelihood (the BHN also predicts its own uncertainty). The substrate is biologically motivated by noradrenergic gain modulation. We demonstrate that posterior variance over the modulator's side-signal produces dissociable behavioural read-outs at three injection sites, with the substrate's behaviour consistent with the noradrenergic-gain-modulation literature.

**Impactful-vs-reasonable position.** Middle / architectural-impactful. The strongest pure-architecture claim the project can defend. Galanti & Wolf 2020 Theorem 4 (in its identifiability reading) is the formal anchor.

**Venue family.** NeurIPS / ICLR / *TMLR* / AABI / NeurIPS BDL workshop.

**Compute / planning cost.** Row EE-6 has to be implemented (non-trivial — a new ensemble head, a heteroscedastic precision head, the ρ-initialisation choice, the variance-space disambiguation; see [investigation memo §3](../concepts/film_hypernet_bnn_lineage_and_critic_modulation.md)) on top of v5's §5 experimental program. Order of magnitude: 4–6 weeks of senior-developer + developer time, plus the experiments themselves.

**Submission timeline.** 3–5 months wall-clock. Behind Path A by ~6 weeks because of Row EE-6 implementation.

**Risk profile.** Architectural-implementation risk lives on Row EE-6. The neuromodulation professor flagged that this framing risks reading as "post-hoc rationalisation of a choice motivated by target-specific gain on a multifunctional modulator" — the paper would have to answer that in the discussion. The project's downstream pain identity sits in the motivation only, weakening the bridge to follow-up clinical work.

**Map to v5's body.** Path B *promotes* the Row EE-6 architectural artefact from §3.0 / §3.5 lens framing to the paper's headline. v5 §5 experimental program is unchanged but augmented by Row EE-6 ablations. The substrate-level claim is reformulated as "first instantiation of an approximate Bayesian Hypernetwork with heteroscedastic likelihood for an RL policy", with LC-NA in motivation.

### Path C — NA-targeted plus conditional pain-construct upgrade

**Headline (plain English).** Path A's headline, plus a soft second claim: the post-injury behavioural signature of the modulated agent is *consistent with* the kinds of multi-effect modulation the predictive-coding-of-pain framework predicts for hypervigilance (Wiech 2016; Tabor & Burr 2019). Specifically, the modulator's persistent state can produce avoidance behaviour in the absence of injury risk — a dissociation the predictive-coding-of-pain literature identifies as the signature of a prior-shift separable from the input.

**Impactful-vs-reasonable position.** Reasonable on the substrate claim; soft-impactful on the pain claim, conditional on the probe-trial extension landing.

**Venue family.** If the probe-trial lands: *PAIN* / *Nature Mental Health* / *Computational Psychiatry*. If not: the Path A venue list.

**Compute / planning cost.** Path A's full cost, plus the post-recovery predator-cue probe-trial extension (v5 §5.8 — a "small senior-developer add" per the pain-modeling memo, but it requires a new task variant, a new logged quantity, and the C1 EMA-of-nociception baseline as a comparator); five-seed minimum for the within-trial cross-domain coupling threshold; the C1 baseline run; the four-dissociation gate (v5 §5.7 behaviourally-defined form) pre-registered. Order of magnitude: Path A's wall-clock plus 1–3 months.

**Submission timeline.** 4–7 months wall-clock.

**Risk profile.** Double-conditional. Path A is the floor (the paper publishes regardless). Path C upgrades to a clinical-translational venue *if* the probe-trial lands cleanly *and* the four-dissociation gate passes on five seeds. If either fails, the paper retreats to Path A's headline mid-write.

**Map to v5's body.** Path C *promotes* v5 §5.7's behaviourally-defined gate from gate-keeping to conditional headline. v5 §5.8 probe-trial extension lifts from "small senior-developer add, not committed" to "committed pre-submission". §5.7's verdict promotes from "pending" to "tested".
