# Predictions for v3 direction memo §5 — Bayesian-brain / precision-coding side

## Plain-English entry point

This memo proposes seven predictions for §5 of the v3 NMN-as-scaling-and-shifting direction memo, written from a Bayesian-brain / precision-coding lens that the other three professors are unlikely to bring. The lens reads the project's modulator (a small side-network that watches state and emits scale/shift signals into the main policy) as **a precision-weighting machine on a hierarchical generative model**: the modulator's output is not just a re-scaling of activations, it is the agent's running estimate of how trustworthy each information stream is, given the agent's current beliefs about the world. Under this reading, the *behavioural* signatures that should track the modulator are not just an exploration spike or a learning-rate change — they are signal-detection-theoretic quantities (sensitivity d′, criterion shifts), Bayesian-surprise quantities (KL between value-head posteriors before and after a regime change), and active-inference quantities (epistemic vs pragmatic balance under expected free energy). The locked v2 thesis — Doya-α for ACh, FiLM as forward-pass gain — is not contested; instead this lens **adds** five tests that the locked thesis predicts but does not explicitly stake. The last two predictions are optional and stake more aggressive Bayesian-brain claims (KL-tracking and a precision-vs-α dissociation design). On the v2 §3 ACh-branch decision, my verdict: **the Doya-α choice is fine for v3** — but predictions P3 and P7 below give v3 (or v4) a clean way to reopen the question with data.

## Predictions

### P1 — Precision vs learning-rate dissociation under heteroscedastic noise

- **Claim.** If the modulator is implementing precision-weighting in the Yu & Dayan 2005 sense (likelihood precision modulated by expected uncertainty), the behavioural signature should be a **sensitivity (d′) shift, not a learning-rate shift**, when the same agent faces *two different sources* of noise: observation noise (which precision should down-weight) and reward-stochasticity (which α should track). Under the Doya-α reading these should be conflated — α responds to TD-error magnitude regardless of source. Under a precision reading the modulator response at injection site A (encoder) should decouple: high obs-noise → low likelihood precision (lower γ at A), reward stochasticity → high α only.
- **Bayesian / generative-model grounding.** Yu & Dayan 2005 (*Neuron*) explicitly distinguishes *expected uncertainty* (within-context noise, ACh territory) from *unexpected uncertainty* (cross-context change, NA territory). Friston 2010 (*Nat Rev Neurosci*) Eq. 4 writes the free-energy gradient with explicit precision-weighting: $\partial_\mu F = \Pi \cdot \varepsilon$, with $\Pi$ the precision matrix and $\varepsilon$ the prediction error. Knill & Pouget 2004 (*Trends Neurosci*) frames cortical activity as a population code for posterior uncertainty; gain modulation re-parameterises which sensory channels enter the posterior with what weight. Shine 2021 Box 3 (in-corpus, reviewed) makes the canonical neuromodulator-to-precision mapping: ACh ↔ precision on bottom-up prediction errors. The Doya-α and Yu-Dayan-precision readings are *not* the same machine — α controls value-update step size, $\Pi$ controls likelihood-weighting in the posterior — but their behavioural readouts under reward-stochasticity-only environments are partially overlapping.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Construct two contrasting regimes on the existing grid-world testbed: (i) **high obs-noise / clean reward** (predator-position observation corrupted by Gaussian noise; reward deterministic), and (ii) **clean obs / stochastic reward** (full obs; Bernoulli reward conditional on action). Measure peri-switch (a) modulator response at injection site A vs site C, and (b) d′ on a forced-choice probe (see P5). Prediction: site-A response is large under regime (i) and small under regime (ii); site-C response shows the opposite pattern.
- **Falsification signature.** Modulator response at A and C move together under both regimes → modulator is a context-detector with redundant heads (Vecoven-2020-style), precision reading refuted, Doya-α reading holds. This is also the "demote to context detector" outcome both v1 reviewers already flagged for prediction (d).

### P2 — Active-inference / expected-free-energy temperature reading at injection C

- **Claim.** Under active inference, the policy's action selection is driven by a softmax over expected free energy $G(\pi)$ at inverse-temperature $\gamma_G$: $\pi \propto \exp(-\gamma_G \cdot G(\pi))$. Expected free energy decomposes into a **pragmatic** term (expected log-preference under the policy) and an **epistemic** term (expected information gain about hidden states). If the modulator at site C is controlling $\gamma_G$ rather than the standard PPO inverse-temperature, the behavioural signature is **information-seeking under high observation noise, not just under regime change**: the agent should choose actions that resolve hidden-state uncertainty even when those actions cost expected reward.
- **Bayesian / generative-model grounding.** Friston, Rigoli et al. 2015 "Active inference and epistemic value" (*Cognitive Neuroscience*) derives the EFE decomposition: $G(\pi) = \underbrace{-\mathbb{E}_{q(o,s|\pi)}\!\left[\ln p(o)\right]}_{\text{pragmatic}} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q(o|\pi)}\!\left[\mathrm{KL}(q(s|o,\pi)\,\|\,q(s|\pi))\right]}_{\text{epistemic}}$. Pezzulo, Rigoli & Friston 2015 (*Prog Neurobiol*) maps the precision on EFE onto noradrenergic / cholinergic gain. Parr & Friston 2017 (*Sci Rep* "The active construction of the visual world") gives the policy-temperature reading explicitly. None of these are in-corpus; the postdoc may decide whether to add Friston/Pezzulo/Parr to the reference set or leave as future work.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Augment the testbed with an **information-action**: a costly "look" action that resolves predator-position uncertainty for the next $k$ steps. Measure the agent's rate of choosing the look action as a function of (a) regime-change distance and (b) observation noise level. Log the modulator's site-C output alongside. Prediction: site-C output and look-action rate co-vary; the modulator's response to high noise is *larger than* its response to TD-error of equal magnitude under a reward-perturbation control. The asymmetry is the active-inference signature.
- **Falsification signature.** Look-action rate tracks TD-error magnitude identically under reward perturbation and observation noise → modulator is doing standard PPO-temperature, not EFE-temperature. This is the cleanest dissociation between "v2 Doya-NA-β at C" and "active-inference $\gamma_G$ at C".

### P3 — Level-specific precision across the three injection sites

- **Claim.** Under hierarchical predictive coding, a generative model has *level-specific precisions*: sensory precision $\Pi_s$ (how much to trust the observation), state precision $\Pi_z$ (how much to trust the prior over hidden states), and policy precision $\Pi_\pi$ (how sharply to commit to one policy over another). The project's three injection sites map to these levels almost exactly: **A (encoder) ↔ sensory precision $\Pi_s$; B (GRU update gate) ↔ state-transition precision $\Pi_z$; C (policy temperature) ↔ policy precision $\Pi_\pi$**. The prediction is that the three sites' modulator responses should be **dissociable in their triggers**: A responds to *observation noise*, B responds to *state-prediction-error magnitude*, C responds to *value-prediction-error magnitude*.
- **Bayesian / generative-model grounding.** Adams, Shipp & Friston 2013 (*Brain Struct Funct* "Predictions not commands") lays out the canonical hierarchical-precision message-passing scheme; Shine 2021 §2.7 and Box 3 (in-corpus) explicitly identify precision with population variance and neuromodulators with the gain on prediction-error terms at each level: $F = \tfrac{1}{2}\Pi(\mu_{\text{sensory}} - g(\mu_{\text{state}}))^2 + \dots$ with $\Pi$ tuned per level by ascending neuromodulator concentrations. Costacurta 2024 (in-corpus) Fig. 3F shows the *dissociation-by-ablation* signature this prediction operationalises — the corpus's closest precedent for site-specific modulator function. Wainstein 2025 (in-corpus) gives the empirical anchor: site-specific gain (entropy-driven, restricted to one population) produces dissociable network-trajectory signatures.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Per-site partial regression: regress site-A $\gamma$ on (observation-noise magnitude, state-prediction-error magnitude, value-prediction-error magnitude); same for site B and site C. The level-specific-precision prediction is that each site loads disproportionately on its level-matched regressor. The matrix of regression coefficients across sites × triggers should be approximately diagonal under the precision reading; off-diagonal under a single-channel-with-redundant-heads reading.
- **Falsification signature.** All three sites load equally on all three regressors → modulator is doing one-channel work fanned out, level-specific precision is refuted, and the v2 reviewers' "NA with two passenger heads" reading wins. This requires the **Phase 0 T/P split** to be testable — without two timescales the three sites are forced to correlate and the regression is degenerate. v3 §5 should note this conditional.

### P4 — Drift-diffusion / signal-detection signature at site C

- **Claim.** At the behaviour level, modulator-mediated gain change at injection site C should produce a **shift in the agent's signal-detection sensitivity (d′) under a forced-choice discrimination probe, not just a shift in criterion**. Under a precision reading, raising likelihood precision sharpens the posterior over hidden states — equivalent to higher d′. Under a pure inverse-temperature reading (Doya-NA-β), the modulator only changes the softmax sharpness — equivalent to a criterion shift with d′ fixed. **The two are formally distinct: d′ measures information content of the policy's underlying state estimate; criterion measures decision threshold given that estimate.** This is the standard signal-detection-theoretic decomposition (Green & Swets 1966).
- **Bayesian / generative-model grounding.** Signal detection theory's d′ = $z(H) - z(F)$ (z-transformed hit rate minus false-alarm rate) is the operational measure of underlying-state-estimate quality, independent of decision policy. In a Bayesian generative model, d′ tracks posterior precision; criterion tracks utility-weighted decision threshold. Friston 2010 §"Hierarchical Inference" and Knill & Pouget 2004 §3 both make this distinction explicit; the latter notes that perceptual hypervigilance can manifest as either a *prior shift* (criterion) or a *likelihood-precision shift* (d′), and that the two have distinct empirical signatures. Wainstein 2025 (in-corpus) §2.1 already implements an entropy-driven gain ODE that operationalises the precision arm; the same machinery in PPO would predict an analogous d′ shift.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Construct a forced-choice probe: at fixed grid positions, the agent is asked to commit to one of two predator-presence hypotheses based on a single observation. Sweep observation noise to define a d′ curve. Compare four conditions: (i) baseline modulator on; (ii) γ-clamp at C (multiplicative arm frozen); (iii) β-clamp at C (additive arm frozen); (iv) modulator-off. Prediction: γ-clamp degrades d′ specifically; β-clamp shifts criterion but preserves d′. Under the v2 §5 (b′) reading (gamma-arm = inverse-temperature) this is a *criterion shift only* and d′ is preserved across all conditions — the precision reading is differentially refuted.
- **Falsification signature.** d′ is unchanged by all four conditions and only criterion moves → modulator does not implement likelihood-precision; the v2 Doya-NA-β-at-C reading holds and the precision reading is refuted. This is a *one-shot dissociation* of the precision vs inverse-temperature accounts at the same injection site.

### P5 — Modulator response under expected vs unexpected uncertainty (Yu-Dayan signature)

- **Claim.** Yu & Dayan 2005 makes a *bidirectional* prediction: ACh tracks **expected uncertainty** (variance within a known context); NA tracks **unexpected uncertainty** (extra variance signalling context change). If the project's modulator carries an ACh-like component, its phasic response under sustained-but-known noise should be **suppressed** relative to its response at a regime change of equal local TD-error magnitude. The two stimuli produce equal $|\delta|$ but the ACh-like component should distinguish them; a pure NA/TD-error tracker should not.
- **Bayesian / generative-model grounding.** Yu & Dayan 2005 §"Two types of uncertainty" makes the dissociation explicit; Lee 2024 (in-corpus) Eq. 4 operationalises it as $\alpha = E/(E+A)$ and $\beta^{-1} = \langle E\rangle$ — the *expected* uncertainty $E$ gates learning rate, the *unexpected* uncertainty enters via the inverse-temperature. Sales et al. 2019 (Yu-Dayan extended to active inference; not in-corpus) and Pezzulo & Rigoli 2015 (not in-corpus) extend this to hierarchical generative models. The dissociation has a clean operational test on the project's testbed.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Two-arm comparison. **Arm 1** — sustained high-noise environment without context switch (high expected uncertainty $E$, low unexpected uncertainty $A$). **Arm 2** — regime change with matched local TD-error magnitude (low $E$, high $A$). Per-step modulator response logged. Prediction: under a Yu-Dayan reading, response at site A (ACh-analogue) is high in Arm 1 and low in Arm 2; response at site C (NA-analogue) is the opposite. Under v2 §3's chosen "all sites driven by single mod_h" reading without T/P split, the two arms should produce identical responses — and this would refute the precision reading.
- **Falsification signature.** Identical per-site modulator response across Arm 1 and Arm 2 → no expected/unexpected dissociation; the modulator does not carry distinct precision and learning-rate-shift signals; the v2 Doya-α-only reading is supported.

### P6 — Bayesian-surprise / KL-divergence tracking (optional, more aggressive)

- **Claim.** Under a strict Bayesian-brain reading, the modulator's phasic burst at a regime change should correlate not with $|\delta|$ (TD-error magnitude) but with **Bayesian surprise** $\mathrm{KL}(q_{\text{post}} \,\|\, q_{\text{prior}})$ between the agent's belief state before and after the observation. TD-error is a *scalar reward-related residual*; Bayesian surprise is a *full-distribution shift*. The two are equal only in degenerate Gaussian-with-fixed-variance cases. A modulator that tracks $\mathrm{KL}$ but not $|\delta|$ in cases where they dissociate is a *Bayesian-surprise tracker*; one that tracks $|\delta|$ but not $\mathrm{KL}$ is a *TD-error tracker*. v2 prediction (d) only tests the latter.
- **Bayesian / generative-model grounding.** Itti & Baldi 2009 (*Vision Research*) and Friston, Daunizeau & Kiebel 2009 (*Free energy and active inference*) define Bayesian surprise as the KL between posterior and prior, distinct from Shannon surprise $-\log p(o)$. Schwartenbeck et al. 2015 (*Cerebral Cortex* "The Dopaminergic midbrain encodes the expected certainty about desired outcomes") suggests phasic dopamine encodes a precision-weighted prediction error that, in a generative-model framing, *is* a Bayesian-surprise-like quantity. The project's modulator at site C (driving policy) is the natural place to test this — the value-head's distribution before and after a regime-change observation is what supports the KL computation.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Construct two conditions where $|\delta|$ and $\mathrm{KL}(V_\text{post} \| V_\text{prior})$ dissociate. Example: (i) **high $|\delta|$, low KL** — a large but expected reward shift that the value head was already prepared for (prior mean off but variance high); (ii) **low $|\delta|$, high KL** — a small reward shift that contradicts a confidently-held prior (prior mean close but variance low). The agent's value-head distribution gives both quantities directly (PPO maintains state-conditional value baselines). Regress modulator burst magnitude on both. Prediction under strict Bayesian reading: KL coefficient significant, $|\delta|$ coefficient null or small. Under v2 Doya reading: $|\delta|$ coefficient significant, KL coefficient subsumed.
- **Falsification signature.** $|\delta|$ subsumes KL in the regression → modulator is a TD-error tracker, not a Bayesian-surprise tracker; the strict Bayesian reading is **demoted**, the v2 Doya-NA / Doya-α reading is supported. This is a useful negative result — it tells the project not to overclaim the Bayesian-brain framing for v3.

### P7 — Re-opening the ACh-precision vs ACh-α branch decision (optional)

- **Claim.** v2 §3 explicitly chooses the Doya-α branch for ACh over the Yu-Dayan precision branch. From the Bayesian-brain lens, **the cleanest experimental design that would force v4 to reopen this choice is the combined P1 + P4 test** — site-A modulator response under heteroscedastic noise (P1) *plus* the d′/criterion decomposition under γ-vs-β clamp at site A (P4-adapted). If site-A response selectively tracks observation noise (not reward stochasticity), AND γ-clamp at A degrades d′ (not just criterion), the modulator at A is doing precision work, not learning-rate work. If either test fails the precision side, Doya-α is the right choice for v4 too.
- **Bayesian / generative-model grounding.** The two readings disagree on which behaviour is the *primary* signature. Doya-α predicts post-switch adaptation-speed changes (a learning-rate signature); Yu-Dayan precision predicts within-noise discrimination changes (a precision signature). The two are not mutually exclusive in principle — a single modulator could do both at different sites — but the v2 commitment is at A specifically and is testable specifically there. Lee 2024 (in-corpus) §4 already combines both readings into a "Doya-DaYu" agent; the project's testbed would be the first end-to-end-learned operationalisation that decides between them rather than averaging.
- **Project-testbed measure.** See P1 and P4. The conjunction of (a) site-A response selectively triggered by observation noise and (b) γ-at-A clamp degrading d′ is the precision side's joint sufficient signature.
- **Falsification signature.** Either (a) site-A response is equal under observation-noise and reward-stochasticity, or (b) γ-at-A clamp shifts criterion only — the precision reading at A is refuted, and the v2 Doya-α choice is supported as principled and final. **My verdict for v3: keep Doya-α; design v3's experiments such that P1 + P4 conjunction can be measured but is not on the critical path. The data the testbed yields will tell v4 whether to reopen.**

## Methodological notes

These predictions require operational additions to the testbed that the senior-developer and experiment-designer will need to land. None require changes to the modulator architecture beyond the T/P split that v2 already escalates to prerequisite. The additions:

- **A forced-choice discrimination probe** (P4, P7) — a periodic "stop and commit" decision interleaved with the standard navigation task, where the agent must report a hidden-state hypothesis (predator-present vs absent) from a single noisy observation. Gives d′ and criterion on a per-episode basis. Probably the highest-cost addition.
- **An information-action ("look")** (P2) — a costly, no-movement action that returns a denoised observation for $k$ subsequent steps. Required for the EFE / epistemic-value reading.
- **Heteroscedastic-noise variants of existing stages** (P1, P5) — a high-obs-noise / clean-reward variant and a clean-obs / stochastic-reward variant of one of the existing predator-avoidance stages. Two new YAML configs; no architecture change.
- **Value-head distribution logging** (P6) — log not just the value-head mean but its full predicted distribution (variance suffices for KL under a Gaussian approximation). Probably already logged for PPO advantage; check with senior-developer.
- **Per-site partial regression on (obs-noise, state-PE, value-PE)** (P3) — a post-hoc analysis pipeline on already-logged FiLM γ, β values. No new logging if site-level FiLM outputs are already saved; otherwise add a logging hook.

The Phase 0 T/P split (v2 §3 prerequisite) is required for P1, P3, P5, P7 to be identifiable; without it, single mod_h forces correlated movement across sites and the regressions are degenerate.
