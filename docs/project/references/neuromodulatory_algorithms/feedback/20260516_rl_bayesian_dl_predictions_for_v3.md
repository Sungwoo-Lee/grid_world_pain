# Predictions for v3 direction memo §5 — RL / Bayesian-DL / architectural side

## Plain-English entry point

The project's claim is that one small side-network (the **NMN** — a circuit that watches the agent's state and emits two signals, a multiplicative "scale" $\gamma$ and an additive "shift" $\beta$, which are then injected into the main policy network via a **FiLM** operation $h \mapsto \gamma \odot h + \beta$) is doing two jobs at once: at the cellular level it is rescaling each target neuron's input-output slope (the textbook definition of "neural gain"), and at the behaviour level it is retuning standard reinforcement-learning hyperparameters (how fast the agent learns, how much it explores, how sharply the policy commits to a chosen action). The reference corpus contains many papers that argue *one* side of that bridge but none that have run the symmetric two-direction test on the same agent on the same trials. The predictions below — written from the architectural / RL-hyperparameter side of the bridge — are the falsifiable claims the project's three-injection-site FiLM substrate can carry into v3 §5. Each prediction names (a) the corpus paper that supports it, (b) the section / equation / figure of that paper, and (c) a concrete WandB-scalar-level measure the project can run on its current testbed.

## Predictions

### P-1 The actor-site FiLM substrate is, today, behaving as a Noradrenergic / inverse-temperature controller with two passenger heads

- **Claim.** Under the project's current single-`mod_h` recurrent design, the FiLM $\gamma$ injected at Injection C (policy logits) acts as a state-dependent inverse-temperature scalar $\beta_{\text{eff}}$ in the actor softmax. The other two injection sites (A encoder, B GRU update gate) are *passenger heads* — they share the same scalar source, so their outputs cannot dissociate from the C-site read-out without the Phase 0 T/P split. This is the cleanest single-`mod_h` reading.
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Doya 2002 §3.3 — NA modulates the softmax inverse temperature $\beta$, mathematically equivalent to multiplicatively rescaling the actor's logits. Lee et al. 2024 Eq. 4 — `$\beta^{-1}(s) = 1/\langle E(s, \hat a)\rangle_{\hat a}$` makes the same identification, hand-coded. Vecoven et al. 2020 Fig. 7 — when a scalar-$z$ neuromodulator drives a multi-site FiLM, dimensions go un-recruited on simple benchmarks; the recruitment pattern is diagnostic.
- **Project-testbed measure.** At Injection C, regress effective actor-softmax inverse-temperature (computed from the logit norm post-FiLM) on `mod_h` across regime-change episodes; correlation should exceed correlation at sites A and B by a factor of two or more. **Predicted WandB scalar**: `mod_h_to_beta_eff_R2` at C ≥ 0.5 while the same scalar at A and B remains < 0.3.
- **Falsification signature.** If the regression coefficient is comparable across all three sites, `mod_h` is not behaving as a C-localised temperature controller but as a global volume knob the rest of the network absorbs — and the v3 contribution shrinks to "we built a Vecoven-style NMN".

### P-2 Effective-rank of $\gamma$ at the actor site collapses to 1 under the Doya-channel reading

- **Claim.** If the FiLM $\gamma$ at Injection C is implementing a single Doya hyperparameter (inverse temperature, per P-1), then across an evaluation distribution that spans regime changes, the singular-value spectrum of the $\gamma$-output matrix should be dominated by a single mode. A high-rank $\gamma$ is *over-parameterised relative to the Doya knob* — fine if the optimiser collapses the extra degrees of freedom, problematic if it does not.
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Vecoven et al. 2020 Fig. 7 — the $z$-recruitment plot is the direct precedent; many $z$ dimensions go un-recruited on simple benchmarks, and the recruitment pattern is diagnostic of "how many effective scalars the network actually uses". Costacurta et al. 2024 Eq. 4 and Fig. 3F — low-rank weight scaling with $K$ rank-1 components is the natural structured-flexibility intermediate; their Fig. 3F shows dissociable ablation only when the modulator has learned to dedicate different rank-1 components to different sub-computations.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Stack $\gamma^{(C)}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ across an evaluation rollout of $T$ steps spanning multiple regime changes; compute SVD; report the *effective rank* $r_{\text{eff}}(\gamma^{(C)}) = (\sum_i \sigma_i)^2 / \sum_i \sigma_i^2$. **Predicted WandB scalar**: `gamma_C_effective_rank` ≤ 2.0. Same probe at sites A and B for contrast.
- **Falsification signature.** If `gamma_C_effective_rank` ≥ 5, the FiLM is doing something Doya 2002 does not describe — it is closer to Vecoven's full $z^\top(x w_s + w_b)$ machine in its un-collapsed form, and the v3 claim should be "multi-dimensional gain controller", not "RL-hyperparameter modulator". If the rank stays high *and* P-3's ablation succeeds, that combination is the project's strongest signal that an emergent multi-channel mapping has been learned that Lee 2024's hand-coded scalar cannot capture.

### P-3 Per-injection-site clamps produce dissociable hyperparameter-distinguishable deficits — Costacurta Fig. 3F analogue

- **Claim.** Clamping the FiLM at one site at a time ($\gamma=1, \beta=0$ at A, then B, then C, three separate runs) should degrade *different* behavioural quantities: an A-clamp should degrade performance under noisy observations (learning-rate-sensitive, Doya-$\alpha$); a C-clamp should degrade post-regime-change recovery and entropy-spike behaviour (NA-$\beta$); a B-clamp should degrade tasks that need protected memory across switches (effective discount or memory-stability).
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Costacurta et al. 2024 Fig. 3F — the published precedent for dissociation-by-ablation in a multi-dimensional neuromodulator. Their Prop. 1 (LSTM-equivalence) shows that when the modulator gates the recurrent weight, each gated rank-1 component can carry a distinct computational sub-step. Doya 2002 §3 — the four-system anatomical partition predicts dissociable behavioural roles. Predictions synthesis §3.5.4 names this as a gap the project's three-site FiLM is positioned to fill.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Three single-site-clamp conditions × seeds, evaluated on (a) survival-step under heterogeneous noise, (b) survival-step recovery half-life after stage switch, (c) survival-step on memory-required predator-avoidance. **Predicted WandB scalars**: `delta_survival_clamp_A` largest on noisy-obs task; `delta_survival_clamp_C` largest on regime-change task; `delta_survival_clamp_B` largest on memory-required task.
- **Falsification signature.** If a single clamp (most likely C, per P-1) dominates all three task-axes — i.e. one clamp degrades everything and the other two clamps degrade nothing — the multi-channel claim collapses and `mod_h` is provably a single-channel signal with redundant heads. This is the operational null for the "three-site FiLM is a multi-channel modulator" claim and is the strongest single-experiment refutation of v3.

### P-4 The combined two-stage falsifier (b′ + $|\delta|$-tracking) — three distinguishable outcome scenarios

- **Claim.** The v2 memo's combined falsifier (freeze $\gamma=1$ vs $\beta=0$ at Injection C, then test whether the surviving arm's activity tracks the magnitude of the temporal-difference error $|\delta|$) produces three discriminating outcomes that map cleanly onto three different theoretical readings:
  - **Outcome U (unification holds at C).** $\gamma$-freeze is more damaging than $\beta$-freeze AND the surviving $\beta$-arm's activity correlates with $|\delta|$. Reading: $\gamma$ is doing Doya-NA work (multiplicative-on-logits temperature) at C, and the additive arm carries the TD-error-driven hypertube-origin shift. The unification claim holds at C.
  - **Outcome V (Vecoven context-conditioning, no Doya channel).** Both freezes are roughly equally damaging AND neither arm tracks $|\delta|$. Reading: the modulator is detecting *task identity* (Vecoven 2020's freeze-$z$ experiment, §4.4-4.5), not signalling a Doya hyperparameter. The project's contribution shrinks to "FiLM works on PPO".
  - **Outcome T (Tsuda hypertube dynamics dominate).** $\beta$-freeze is more damaging than $\gamma$-freeze. Reading: the additive arm is producing activity-space displacements (Tsuda et al. 2021 Fig. 3 hypertubes, even though their *mechanism* is multiplicative-on-weights) and the multiplicative arm is not load-bearing for the regime-change adaptation. The Doya / RL-hyperparameter framing should be walked back; gain control is the right story, hyperparameter modulation is not.
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Vecoven et al. 2020 §4.4-4.5 — the freeze-$z$ experiment is the published precedent for Outcome V. Tsuda et al. 2021 Fig. 3 + Extended Data Appendix A — the hypertube-shift precedent for Outcome T (with the caveat that Tsuda's mechanism is multiplicative-on-weights, not additive-on-activations, so this is an analogy, not a direct mapping). Lee et al. 2024 Eq. 4 — the Doya-NA reading for Outcome U.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Four-arm experiment at the regime-change test stage: full FiLM, $\gamma=1$ clamp at C, $\beta=0$ clamp at C, both clamps. Per arm, log (i) survival-step recovery half-life, (ii) actor-entropy peri-switch spike amplitude, (iii) Pearson correlation of surviving FiLM-arm output with $|\delta|$ over the recovery window. **Predicted WandB scalars**: `recovery_halflife_per_clamp`, `entropy_spike_amplitude_per_clamp`, `r_filmarm_vs_abs_delta_per_clamp`.
- **Falsification signature.** Outcome V or Outcome T refutes the unification claim as written; Outcome U is the headline-defensible result. A four-way tie — all clamps perform the same — refutes the FiLM machinery as load-bearing at all, and the project should re-investigate whether the modulator's training signal reaches Injection C.

### P-5 The learned `mod_h`-to-FiLM mapping reorganises through training to approximate Lee 2024's hand-coded Component IV — with the mapping recoverable post-hoc

- **Claim.** Where Lee et al. 2024 hand-codes the cellular-to-hyperparameter mapping ($\alpha = E / (E+A)$, $\beta^{-1} = \langle E\rangle$ — Eq. 4), the project's contribution is to *let the mapping emerge from gradient descent on the PPO objective* and recover it post-hoc. The prediction: when the FiLM-augmented agent has reached its final policy on a non-stationary task family, regressing the FiLM $\gamma^{(C)}$ on ensemble-derived aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty estimates should recover a relationship of the same *sign and qualitative shape* as Lee Eq. 4, *without that mapping having been trained-in*. A failed regression — small $R^2$, no monotonic relationship — would tell us the learned mapping has found a different solution to the same problem (the Doya-channel mapping is one solution; not the unique one).
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Lee et al. 2024 §3.1 + Fig. 2 — the four-component framework. Lee Eq. 4 — the specific functional forms. Lee §4 (Doya-DaYu agent) — the bandit demonstration. Vecoven et al. 2020 §4 (adaptation) — the precedent for "the modulator's output is interpretable post-hoc". Mei et al. 2022 Fig. 1B — the conceptual canonical for "neuromodulation → hyperparameter fine-tuning emerging in a DNN".
- **Project-testbed measure.** After training, run a PPO ensemble (4-8 seeds, or a single-network with bootstrap re-sampling) to estimate aleatoric uncertainty $A(s,a)$ (return-distribution variance per arm) and epistemic uncertainty $E(s,a)$ (across-ensemble disagreement on $\mathbb{E}[Q]$). Regress per-step $\gamma^{(C)}$ on $E/(E+A)$ and per-step inverse-effective-temperature on $1/\langle E\rangle$; report $R^2$ and sign. **Predicted WandB scalars**: `mod_h_lee_alpha_recovery_R2`, `mod_h_lee_beta_recovery_R2`, both ≥ 0.4 with positive sign.
- **Falsification signature.** If $R^2 < 0.1$ for both regressions, the learned mapping is not Lee-equivalent and the project must report what it *is* — possibly a context detector (Outcome V of P-4), possibly a TD-error-magnitude tracker (P-7), possibly something the corpus does not yet name. A negative-result here is publishable: it would be the first principled disagreement between an end-to-end-learned modulator and the leading hand-coded mapping in the field.

### P-6 The forward-pass FiLM has no analogue of Rodriguez-Garcia's curvature reparameterisation — and this is testable as a robustness signature

- **Claim.** Rodriguez-Garcia et al. 2026 §2.1 + Appendix B proves that their gain-modulated SGD reparameterises the loss-landscape curvature as $\lambda_i^{\text{eff}} = \lambda_i / g^2$. This is an *optimiser-level* effect — the gain multiplies the *gradient*. The project's FiLM multiplies the *forward activation* and leaves PPO's Adam untouched. **Prediction: the project's FiLM-augmented agent should *not* show Rodriguez-Garcia-style loss-landscape flattening around regime changes**, because the mechanism that produces it is absent.
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Rodriguez-Garcia et al. 2026 §2.1, Eq. 2; Algorithm 1; Fig. 2 (loss-landscape flattening). The convergent professor note (v1 §8) explicitly named this orthogonality: "Rodriguez-Garcia modulates the *gradient* / optimiser-level; this project modulates the *forward activation* / function approximator". Predictions synthesis §5.2 names the orthogonality as a publishable gap.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Around each regime change, compute the top-$k$ eigenvalues of the empirical Hessian of the PPO loss over a small batch (Hessian-vector products via `jax.hessian` are tractable for a small actor-critic). Compare pre-switch vs in-switch vs post-switch top-eigenvalue trajectories. **Predicted WandB scalars**: `loss_hessian_top1_periswitch`, `loss_hessian_top5_avg_periswitch` — these should be *similar* for the FiLM agent and the unmodulated baseline; both should differ from a Rodriguez-Garcia-style NGM-SGD agent (if implemented as a comparator). A discriminating-measure failure here — i.e. the FiLM agent showing curvature flattening anyway — would mean the forward-pass FiLM is leaking into the optimiser dynamics (a finding worth publishing in its own right).
- **Falsification signature.** Two failure modes. (i) If the FiLM agent *does* show curvature flattening comparable to Rodriguez-Garcia's NGM-SGD, the orthogonality claim in v2 §3 is wrong and the two mechanisms are not as separable as the convergent professor note states. (ii) If the unmodulated baseline shows flattening (PPO's clipping providing it via a different route), the discriminating measure is not load-bearing and a different probe is needed.

### P-7 The phasic/tonic dissociation of `mod_h` is observable only after the Phase 0 T/P split — and is the project's strongest single-substrate gap-filling result

- **Claim.** Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005's phasic-vs-tonic NA dissociation is foundational background in the corpus (cited by Lee 2024 §3.4, Wainstein 2025, Mei 2022, Rodriguez-Garcia 2026), but no published forward-pass FiLM-style machine in the corpus has two recurrent timescales with separable phasic and tonic readouts. The project's Phase 0 T/P split, if implemented, fills this gap. The prediction: the P recurrent state should track $|\delta|$ phasically with a time-constant of order one episode; the T recurrent state should track cumulative cost or running value-baseline error with a time-constant of tens of episodes. *Both move at regime change*, but with separable autocorrelation profiles.
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Rodriguez-Garcia et al. 2026 §2.1 Eq. 2 — derives the fast/slow decomposition $W = w_{\text{slow}} + w_{\text{fast}}$ from a phasic + tonic gain split; explicitly one-timescale in their implementation and they flag the multi-timescale extension as future work. Predictions synthesis §5.5 names this as a gap. Costacurta et al. 2024 §3 — the slow modulator / fast output coupling $\tau_z \gg \tau_x$ is the structural precedent.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Post-T/P-split, log P-state and T-state autocorrelation across regime-change windows; compute time-constants via exponential-fit. **Predicted WandB scalars**: `mod_h_P_autocorr_tau` ~ 1-5 episodes; `mod_h_T_autocorr_tau` ~ 50-200 episodes. The separation factor `tau_T / tau_P` ≥ 10 is the empirical signature of a successful T/P split.
- **Falsification signature.** If the two recurrent states collapse to similar time-constants after training (`tau_T / tau_P` < 3), the T/P split has not done its job — the two recurrent compartments have been used by the optimiser as a single distributed signal, and the multi-channel claim remains non-identifiable in the v1 §8 sense. This is the operational pre-flight check for the v3 unification claim: **without it, predictions P-1 through P-5 are not testable**, and v3's headline must be conditional on its success.

### P-8 The learned mapping fails (relative to Lee's hand-coded mapping) where the ensemble-derived uncertainty estimates are themselves unreliable

- **Claim.** Lee et al. 2024's hand-coded Component IV ($\alpha = E/(E+A)$) computes $E$ and $A$ explicitly from an ensemble, so the mapping is exactly as good as the ensemble's calibration. The project's learned mapping has *no explicit ensemble* — the modulator extracts a related quantity end-to-end. Prediction: in regimes where ensemble disagreement is poorly calibrated (e.g., early training, miscalibrated Q-distributions), the learned mapping will *succeed* where Lee's hand-coded version *fails*, because the learned version is not bound to the ensemble's calibration error. Conversely, in regimes where the ensemble is well-calibrated, Lee's mapping will be *more interpretable and statistically efficient* than the learned mapping, because it does not have to learn the mapping from gradient signal alone.
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Lee et al. 2024 Eq. 4 + Fig. 3 (the bandit demonstration uses an explicit ensemble for $A$ via the reliability index Eq. 5 and an ensemble for $E$ via across-network variance). The well-known calibration pathology of small ensembles in RL (Osband et al. 2016, cited by Lee §3) — small ensembles systematically under-estimate epistemic uncertainty early in training. Kendall & Gal 2017's heteroscedastic-aleatoric framework — the *aleatoric* arm is recoverable from a single network's predicted variance head, but the *epistemic* arm requires ensemble disagreement.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Train two configurations: (a) Lee-2024-replica with a hand-coded $\alpha = E/(E+A)$ feeding the actor's softmax temperature; (b) the project's FiLM with end-to-end learned $\gamma^{(C)}$. Evaluate both at multiple training-progress checkpoints (10%, 30%, 50%, 100% of training). **Predicted WandB scalars**: `learned_minus_handcoded_survival_step_diff` should be positive at early checkpoints (learned wins under poor ensemble calibration) and approach zero or slightly negative at late checkpoints (hand-coded catches up).
- **Falsification signature.** If the learned mapping *underperforms* the hand-coded mapping at every checkpoint, the project's "learned beats hand-coded" framing is wrong — the contribution should instead be "learned matches hand-coded at lower implementation cost". This is a graceful degradation, not a refutation.

### P-9 Hypernetwork and conditional-batch-norm alternatives are equivalent at the *signature* level — and the dissociating measure is the per-feature gain variance under regime change

- **Claim.** The closest non-FiLM architectural alternatives — a hypernetwork generating the policy's weights from `mod_h` (Ha 2017, with Costacurta 2024 as the rank-constrained intermediate), and conditional batch normalisation (CBN, the FiLM precursor) — would all produce the same behavioural signature (regime-change recovery, entropy spike, dissociable per-site clamps) *if* their gain output is constrained to lie on the same effective manifold as the FiLM $\gamma$. Prediction: the project's specific differentiation from these alternatives is the *per-feature gain variance* of $\gamma$ across regime changes. A hypernet's full-weight modulation produces a higher-dimensional change-of-weights with a smaller per-feature variance; FiLM produces a low-dimensional change with a *larger* per-feature variance.
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** Costacurta et al. 2024 §"Background" (Hypernetworks, Ha 2017, von Oswald 2020) — explicitly positions NM-RNN as a rank-constrained hypernet. Wang et al. 2024 (NeuronML) — the structural-mask alternative is closer to a hypernet than to FiLM. Vecoven et al. 2020 §2 — FiLM is the simplest member of this family; its per-feature read-out matrices $w_s, w_b$ are the "receptor density" analogue of biology, vs. a hypernet's full weight tensor.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Compute $\text{Var}_t[\gamma_i^{(C)}]$ across a regime-change window for each feature index $i$; report the distribution of per-feature variances. The same probe in a (hypothetical, future) hypernet ablation: compute the analogous per-feature-of-the-generated-weight variance. **Predicted WandB scalar**: `gamma_per_feature_variance_distribution_p90 / p10`. FiLM should show high p90/p10 ratio (a few features carry the change); a hypernet should show lower ratio (change distributed across more features).
- **Falsification signature.** If the FiLM agent's per-feature gain variance distribution is uniform (all features moving together), the architecture is collapsing to a Rodriguez-Garcia-style scalar gain $g$ and the high-dimensional FiLM substrate is wasted. This would say "go simpler" — drop FiLM in favour of a 1-scalar gain.

### P-10 The single-`mod_h` design is provably non-identifiable across the three injection sites — and post-training the recovered Doya-channel attribution is unique up to a rotation of the per-site read-out heads

- **Claim.** With a single recurrent `mod_h$ feeding three FiLM sites $\{A, B, C\}$ via three read-out matrices $\{R_A, R_B, R_C\}$, any orthogonal transformation $Q$ applied as $(R_A, R_B, R_C) \to (Q R_A, Q R_B, Q R_C)$ that preserves the projection of `mod_h` onto policy-relevant subspaces produces an *observationally equivalent* agent. Two implications for v3: (i) the Doya-channel attribution prediction in v2 ("A is ACh-α, B is 5-HT-γ, C is NA-β") is *not* recoverable from observation of the trained agent — it is only recoverable up to the rotation $Q$. (ii) The Phase 0 T/P split must add *at least* one additional separable timescale to break this degeneracy.
- **Architectural / RL grounding.** This is the standard mixing-network identifiability problem from variational inference and ICA — any invertible linear remixing of the source channels produces the same observable mixture (Hyvärinen & Oja 2000). The connection to neural networks via Bayesian identifiability is in Pourzanjani et al. 2017 (out-of-corpus). Costacurta et al. 2024's K rank-1 components face the same problem; their Fig. 3F dissociation is only recoverable because the *task* breaks the symmetry. Predictions synthesis §6.3 names this as an identifiability prerequisite.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Train two identical FiLM agents with different random seeds and *different random initialisations of the three read-out matrices*; check whether the recovered channel-to-site attribution (via P-3's per-site clamp signatures) is consistent across seeds. **Predicted WandB scalar**: `cross_seed_channel_attribution_consistency` (Spearman correlation of per-site clamp-deficit profiles across pairs of seeds) — should be high under the identifiability claim (the *task* breaks the symmetry), low under non-identifiability.
- **Falsification signature.** If cross-seed channel attribution is inconsistent (Spearman < 0.3), the Doya-channel attribution is seed-dependent — i.e. site A is NA in some runs and ACh in others — and the project must report aggregate-statistics-over-seeds rather than per-site assignments. This refutes the v2 claim "A is the ACh site" in its strong form but is compatible with a weaker "ACh-α-style behaviour emerges at one of the three sites, with the assignment being seed-specific".

## Methodological notes

The predictions above require the following operational additions to the project's logging and ablation harness; the postdoc and `senior-developer` will need this list.

- **`mod_h` time-aligned logging.** For P-1, P-4, P-5, P-8: log `mod_h`, $\gamma^{(A)}, \gamma^{(B)}, \gamma^{(C)}, \beta^{(A)}, \beta^{(B)}, \beta^{(C)}$, actor entropy, $|\delta|$, and effective inverse-temperature post-FiLM at the same cadence (every step, not just every episode).
- **Per-site clamp harness.** For P-3, P-4: an experiment-time flag that sets $\gamma^{(s)} = 1, \beta^{(s)} = 0$ at one site $s \in \{A, B, C\}$ without modifying any other site, with seeds matched to a full-FiLM baseline. The project's existing `srcip` and FiLM-disable hooks already cover this for the agent-level case; per-site is the new requirement.
- **SVD / effective-rank logging.** For P-2: stack $\gamma^{(C)}_t$ across eval rollouts; SVD; report effective rank `(\sum \sigma_i)^2 / \sum \sigma_i^2`. This is a small offline post-hoc computation, not an in-training cost.
- **Hessian-eigenvalue logging.** For P-6: top-k eigenvalues of the PPO loss Hessian over a small batch every $N$ steps. JAX's `jax.hessian` or Hessian-vector products via `jax.jvp(jax.grad(L))` are tractable for a small actor-critic; cost should be acceptable on the project's nodes.
- **Two-recurrent-state architecture.** For P-7: the Phase 0 T/P split is a *prerequisite* (per the v1 §8 convergent professor note). Implementation is a `senior-developer` item; the projected hyperparameter is a tonic-state time-constant $\tau_T \in [50, 200]$ episodes vs. phasic-state $\tau_P \in [1, 5]$ episodes.
- **Ensemble for hand-coded comparison.** For P-5, P-8: a Lee-2024-replica configuration requires a small actor-ensemble (4-8 networks, parameter-sharing-with-different-final-heads is acceptable per Osband 2016) to compute aleatoric / epistemic uncertainty. This is invasive — adds 4-8× the forward-pass cost — and should be run as a single comparator experiment rather than a default configuration.
- **Constraint on $\gamma$.** Per the v1 §8 minor flag from `professor-neuromodulation`: Rodriguez-Garcia 2026 explicitly bounds $g(t) \geq g_0 \geq 1$. If v3's headline word is "gain", FiLM $\gamma$ should be constrained $\gamma \geq 0$ (softplus). This affects P-1, P-2, P-9.
- **No emoji / no decoration in the WandB scalars.** The predicted scalar names above are predicates — the actual `experiment-designer` and `senior-developer` choose final naming.
