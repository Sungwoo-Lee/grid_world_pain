# Predictions for v3 direction memo §5 — pain-construct-validity side

## Plain-English entry point

The v2 memo's locked thesis is that the project's small modulator side-circuit (which watches the agent's internal state and emits "scale" and "shift" signals into the main policy via a FiLM layer) is *simultaneously* a cellular-level account of biological neural gain and a behaviour-level account of reinforcement-learning-hyperparameter modulation. The v2 predictions section is currently general — it does not yet make pain-specific commitments. This memo proposes six predictions that the v3 author should consider for §5, each grounded in the computational-pain-science literature (predictive coding of pain, fear-avoidance, allostasis, descending modulation). The point is not to turn v3 into a pain paper — it is to give v3 a defensible link from the unification thesis to the project's chosen empirical testbed (a partially-observed grid world with predator-induced injury and a slow recovery window) so that, when the project later claims its results are pain-relevant, that claim survives a *PAIN* / *Nature Mental Health* reviewer. Each prediction names a behavioural measure currently feasible on the testbed and a falsifier that would refute its pain-construct content. The most important contribution is the construct-validity gate-keeping summary at the end — the minimum set of dissociations that distinguishes "this is a pain-model" from "this is a generic RL-modulator on a pain-flavoured testbed".

## Predictions

### P-Pain-1 — Channel-selective hypervigilance, not volume control

- **Claim.** After an injury event (an injury-channel transient driven by predator contact in the grid world), the modulator should raise the encoder-site FiLM scale ($\gamma_A$) on **threat-relevant** input channels (olfaction, visual when carrying predator cues, exteroceptive nociception) *more than* on threat-irrelevant channels (proprioception, satiation). The selectivity contrast — $\Delta\gamma_{\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{N}} \ge 0.2$ on the canonical noise preset — is what makes the post-injury rise count as *hypervigilance* in the clinical sense, not as generic "the modulator turned up the volume".
- **Pain-construct grounding.** Eccleston & Crombez 1999 ("Pain demands attention") and Crombez et al. 2005 define hypervigilance as *concern-dependent attentional bias toward threat cues* — selective by construction. Wiech 2016 (*Science*) and Büchel et al. 2014 (*Neuron*) frame the selectivity as a precision-weighting on threat-relevant prediction errors, not as a global gain change. Lister et al. 2020 (in-corpus, `computational_pain_models_lit_review.md` §3) operationalises mammalian hypervigilance as a *dose-dependent shift in the cost-weighting of a predator-relevant cue*, not as a general behavioural slowdown. The mapping under the v2 thesis: a hyperparameter shift that fails to discriminate channels is consistent with a 1-D temperature change (Doya-NA scalar), which by the v1-§8 convergence note is what the single-`mod_h` design is most likely doing today — and which is the alternative the project must rule out to call its signature "hypervigilance" rather than "post-injury caution".
- **Project-testbed measure.** Per-modality $\gamma_A$ statistics in an event-locked window ($\pm 50$ steps around predator-contact / injury onset), broken down by the 9 modalities the encoder receives (injury, nutrition, satiation, extero-noc, olfaction, collision, proprioception, visual, location — `PRECISION_MODULATION_ARCHITECTURE.md` §2.3). Threshold from `pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §2.2: $\Delta\gamma_{\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{N}} \ge 0.2$ across $\ge 5$ seeds.
- **Falsification signature.** Post-injury $\gamma_A$ rises uniformly across modalities (or rises only on the injury / extero-noc channel itself, which is the input rather than the threat-prediction layer). Under the v2 b' falsifier, $\gamma$-freeze at site A produces equal damage on threat-detection and non-threat tasks → the modulator is acting as a volume knob (FP-1 in `pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §2.3), not as a hypervigilance machine.
- **Construct-validity caveat.** Selectivity alone is necessary but not sufficient. A generic FiLM modulator trained on a task where threat channels happen to be more diagnostic will also learn selective $\gamma_A$ — that is just task-relevance learning. The pain-construct content requires the selectivity to be **time-locked to the interoceptive injury signal, not to the threat input itself** (so that injury rather than predator presence drives the gain). The lag from injury onset to $\gamma_A$ rise (Bayesian-brain memo's pre-registered lag $\le \min(5, 2\tau_{\text{mod}})$) is the dissociation.

### P-Pain-2 — Tonic precision shift (prior-precision elevation, not learning-rate change)

- **Claim.** With the Phase-0 T/P split in place, the **tonic** modulator state should rise across an injury-and-recovery window and stay elevated **after** the injury channel returns to baseline. Operationally: tonic $\gamma_A$ on threat-relevant channels is significantly higher in the first $\sim 200$ post-recovery steps than in pre-injury baseline, and this elevation is *not* accompanied by a sustained change in actor-entropy (which is the inverse-temperature / NA channel and should decay back with the phasic component). Under the v2 unification thesis this is the **prior-precision arm** of the modulator — distinguishable from a learning-rate change because it persists when no learning is occurring (the policy is not updating because the environment has not just changed).
- **Pain-construct grounding.** Büchel et al. 2014 §"The Bayesian Brain and Pain" frame chronic-pain hypervigilance as an *increase in the precision of the threat prior* — the posterior over bodily threat stays elevated because the prior weight grows relative to incoming evidence. Wiech 2016 (Box 1) makes the same argument explicitly: "increases in the precision of the threat-related prior" produce a posterior shift that outlasts the input. Tabor & Burr 2019 ("Bayesian learning models of pain") map this onto the formal prior $\times$ likelihood decomposition. Lister et al. 2020 §"Phase 2" (in-corpus) leaves the Bayesian decomposition explicitly under-determined between prior shift, precision shift, and cost shift — the project can resolve it because it has access to the substrate. *In the v2 thesis vocabulary*: a precision shift at site A is a perception-side gain that is *separable* from a learning-rate change (which would also require gradient flow into the encoder weights) — exactly the differentiation v2 §3 makes from Rodriguez-Garcia 2026.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Tonic-state ($T$ from T/P split) trajectory across the full episode, aligned to injury onset. Two metrics: (i) ratio of mean $\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}}$ in the post-recovery window ($t = t_{\text{inj}} + \tau_{\text{recovery}}$ to $t = t_{\text{inj}} + \tau_{\text{recovery}} + 200$) to mean pre-injury baseline; (ii) survival metric on a probe trial where the agent is re-exposed to the predator cue with no actual injury risk after recovery completes. The clinical analog of (ii) is the Lister et al. 2020 fox-urine route-avoidance assay — the SNI mouse avoids the threat cue more even when no predator is present.
- **Falsification signature.** Tonic $\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}}$ returns to baseline at the same rate as the injury-channel transient, OR it remains elevated together with actor-entropy (both would imply a single tonic state driving everything — the "1-D EMA mimic" that C1 in `pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §3 is designed to detect).
- **Construct-validity caveat.** Long $T$ time-constant alone is not a *prior-precision* claim. The dissociating feature is that the surviving $\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}}$ tracks **expected** threat (the prior) — not unexpected threat (which would be NA / phasic) and not the injury channel itself (which would be input-tracking). Per `pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §5.2: without a cross-context **transfer probe** (the modulator's persistent state generalises to a novel post-recovery context, not just to the same one), this is "recurrent inertia mis-labelled as chronicity", not chronic-pain analog.

### P-Pain-3 — Allostatic gain reduction at high tonic modulator state (descending-modulation analog)

- **Claim.** When the tonic modulator state is in its high regime (Phase-0 T high after multiple injury-and-recovery cycles, as in the R2 continual probe), the **phasic response to a new noxious event should be attenuated** — both the within-event nociceptive transient on `extero_noc` and the post-injury behavioural decrement should be smaller than at the first injury. Operationally: in the R2 continual schedule, the survival-step decrement at the *second* return-to-active stage is smaller than at the *first* (which is what the empirical anchor already showed, with mod's 2nd return $\ge$ 1st by $+5.75$ vs baseline's monotone decay of $-19.79$ — `nmn_r2_continual_h1b_h1c_confirmed_high_margin.md`).
- **Pain-construct grounding.** Endogenous opioid descending modulation (Fields 2004 *Nat Rev Neurosci*; Eippert et al. 2009; Wager & Atlas 2015) is a **gain reduction on incoming nociceptive signals at the dorsal-horn site**, computationally read as a precision-decrease on the nociceptive likelihood given a high prior on the recuperation context (Geuter, Koban & Wager 2017 *Annu Rev Neurosci*; Ongaro & Kaptchuk 2019 *PAIN* on placebo as Bayesian likelihood reweighting). The clinical signature is *placebo analgesia*: identical nociceptive input produces a smaller perceived-pain posterior when prior context is "safe" or "recuperation-mode". Mahajan, Dayan & Seymour 2026 (in-corpus pain lit review, paper 4) formalises this as the "intertwined inference and control" view of post-injury homeostasis — the controller adapts its own input gain as a function of tonic injury-state inference. Under the v2 thesis: site-A scale at the *encoder* on the `extero_noc` channel should be the substrate analog of opioid-mediated dorsal-horn gain reduction.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Across the R2 continual schedule, log per-stage (i) mean `extero_noc` channel reconstruction error variance pre- and post-injury, (ii) the $\gamma_A^{\text{extero-noc}}$ statistic in the same windows, (iii) the magnitude of the survival-step decrement at each return-to-active stage. The prediction: $\gamma_A^{\text{extero-noc}}$ should *fall* over successive injury episodes if the modulator implements descending-modulation-style attenuation, AND the survival decrement should fall together with it. The empirical anchor's monotone-improving recovery on returns 1→2 is already consistent — the question for v3 is whether this is mechanistically the gain-reduction signature.
- **Falsification signature.** $\gamma_A^{\text{extero-noc}}$ stays constant or rises across successive injuries (the modulator only ever turns up gain, never down). Recovery improvement is driven entirely by policy-side learning, with the modulator off-the-critical-path for the across-episode adaptation.
- **Construct-validity caveat.** The R2 win is *consistent with* descending-modulation gain reduction, but it is also consistent with generic continual-learning consolidation (any of the four Durstewitz 2025 mechanisms). To call this "opioidergic-like" the project must show that the attenuation is **specific to the channel carrying the noxious likelihood** (not a global flattening of all gain heads) AND **conditional on the tonic state being high** (not a monotonic function of training time). This is what the T/P split is for.

### P-Pain-4 — Fear-avoidance as sustained policy-entropy reduction (tonic-shift at site C)

- **Claim.** Under the T/P split, the **tonic** component of the modulator at site C (policy-temperature head) should produce a sustained reduction in policy entropy in regions of the state-space spatially proximal to past injury events — a *contextual* not global entropy drop, tied to where injury occurred rather than to the current nociceptive input. The behavioural signature is reduced state-space coverage of those regions, persistent across episodes in the same world.
- **Pain-construct grounding.** Vlaeyen & Linton 2000 (*Pain*) and Vlaeyen, Crombez & Linton 2016 define fear-avoidance as a *sustained tonic shift in action preference toward safe-over-informative choices*, driven by catastrophising about pain rather than by ongoing nociception. Crombez et al. 2005 ties the avoidance to "concern-dependent" modulation — context-conditional, not global. Apkarian, Baliki & Geha 2009 (*Prog Neurobiol*) frames maladaptive avoidance as the failure mode of an otherwise-adaptive controller — fear-avoidance is the behavioural signature of chronic pain in the clinical literature. The L'Haridon & Cañamero 2023 in-corpus paper (`lharidon_canamero_2023_stress_pain.md`) demonstrates the analog in a Khepera robot: cortisol-modulated pain perception produces *sustained* danger-motivation dominance in high-predator environments, with cortisol-as-tonic-state shifting motivation winner-take-all selection even after the predator is out of sight. This is fear-avoidance instantiated. The v2 thesis maps this onto site-C tonic modulation under T/P split: tonic policy-temperature drop → sustained exploitation-of-safe-actions → reduced state-space coverage.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Per-tile visitation entropy or per-region action-distribution entropy, decomposed into pre- and post-injury phases, with the post-injury phase further split into "near past-injury site" vs "far from past-injury site" regions. Predict: tonic site-C output is more negative (lower temperature, lower entropy) in the near-injury region, and this contrast outlasts the injury-channel transient.
- **Falsification signature.** Policy-entropy reduction is uniform across the state-space (global cautiousness, not contextual avoidance) → consistent with the v2 falsifier's "modulator is a context detector with one tonic state". Alternatively, no sustained reduction at all → consistent with the modulator being purely phasic (Wainstein 2025-style switch-detector with no fear-avoidance content).
- **Construct-validity caveat.** Avoidance is fear-avoidance *only if* it is **disproportionate to the current threat level** — Vlaeyen-Linton's clinical signature is that the avoidance persists when objective pain has resolved. The project must dissociate "rational caution" from fear-avoidance by including a probe where injury risk is *demonstrably zero* in the post-recovery window (e.g., predator-removed probe trial) and showing that the tonic site-C entropy reduction persists. Without this dissociation, what looks like fear-avoidance is just optimal policy under residual risk.

### P-Pain-5 — Construct-validity gate-keeping: behavioural signatures that do NOT count

This is the dual of the prior four predictions and is included as an explicit prediction so v3 can declare what its results *will not* claim. The project's testbed (predator-injury-recovery grid world) will produce several behavioural patterns that are tempting to read as pain-relevant but are not — v3 should pre-commit to *not* reading them as such.

- **(P-Pain-5a) Survival increase under modulator on regime change** — does *not* count as pain-relevant. The R2 win (mod beats baseline +107/+132 steps on return-to-active) is consistent with continual-learning consolidation under any of the four Durstewitz 2025 mechanisms; it is consistent with NA-as-context-detector (the Wainstein-2025 reading); it is consistent with the v2 unification thesis at the *RL* level. To claim it as pain-relevant the project needs the four-property joint hypervigilance fingerprint (channel-selective, time-locked, within-trial cross-domain, outlasting the input) from `pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §2.2, not just the survival number.

- **(P-Pain-5b) Modulator burst at injury onset** — does *not* count as pain-relevant on its own. It is the canonical NA / unexpected-uncertainty signature; Wainstein 2025 + Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005 cover it without any pain content. The pain-construct content requires the burst to drive *channel-selective* gain shifts AND to correlate within-trial with policy-side and memory-side shifts at lag $\le 5$ — i.e., the unification at the *behavioural-readout* level, which is what the Bayesian-brain memo's four-property fingerprint operationalises.

- **(P-Pain-5c) Slow recovery of survival after injury** — does *not* count as chronic-pain analog. Slow recovery is consistent with any slow-state architecture (any recurrent module with long time constant). Per `pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §5.2, the chronic-pain claim survives *only if* the modulator's tonic state representation **transfers across rungs without per-rung surgery** (gap G-G) — i.e., the agent is doing something more than carrying recurrent inertia.

- **(P-Pain-5d) Increased aversion to nociceptive input** — does *not* count as pain. Per the Brand & Yancey 1997 / Cox et al. 2006 textbook line carried in `pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §1, increased aversion to nociceptive input is what *reward shaping on `extero_noc`* produces. To claim pain content the project needs **dissociability from the input** — the posterior (the policy + memory + perception state) stays elevated when the nociceptive input falls, OR moves when the prior moves without the input changing. The latter is the placebo analog (see P-Pain-6).

- **(P-Pain-5e) Continual-learning resistance** — does *not* count as pain content. This is the v2 thesis's own redirect: continual-learning improvement is one readout of state-dependent hyperparameter modulation, not of pain. The construct must be carried by the joint hypervigilance fingerprint at the within-trial level.

### P-Pain-6 (Optional) — Placebo-analog: prior-shift produces posterior-shift in the absence of input change

- **Claim.** If the project's testbed is extended with an **expectation manipulation** — e.g., a learned cue that *predicts* an upcoming injury reliably in pre-training, then is presented at test without an injury actually occurring — the modulator should produce a post-cue-pre-injury rise in $\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}}$ and a sustained site-C tonic shift, *driven by the prior alone with no nociceptive input change*. This would be the project's placebo / nocebo analog. The flip side: a cue that predicts *safety* (recovery context) after injury should produce a measurable *reduction* in the post-injury site-A gain — the placebo-analgesia analog.
- **Pain-construct grounding.** Büchel et al. 2014 (*Neuron*) and Geuter, Koban & Wager 2017 (*Annu Rev Neurosci*) formalise placebo as a Bayesian prior shift; the perceived-pain posterior moves because the prior weight changes, even with the nociceptive likelihood held constant. Ongaro & Kaptchuk 2019 (*PAIN*) restates this as the cleanest computational decomposition. Wiech 2016 frames it as the test-bed-of-choice for the predictive-coding-of-pain account. *None of the 18 papers in the project's neuromodulatory algorithms corpus runs the expectation-manipulation experiment under a forward-pass gain modulator* — this would be a genuinely novel test of the v2 unification thesis in the pain construct's home territory.
- **Project-testbed measure.** Requires testbed extension. The minimal extension is a *learned cue → injury* contingency in pre-training and a *cue-only* test trial. Two metrics in the test trial: (i) $\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}}$ trajectory locked to cue onset (predict: rises in the cue-injury contingency, no rise in the cue-no-injury contingency); (ii) downstream policy-entropy at site C in the post-cue window.
- **Falsification signature.** No cue-locked $\gamma_A^{\mathcal{T}}$ change in the absence of injury → the modulator is input-driven, not prior-driven, and the predictive-coding-of-pain framing does not apply.
- **Construct-validity caveat.** This is a *testbed extension*, not a current-testbed measure. v3 should flag it as future-work-or-design-question, not as a near-term prediction. The current testbed does not natively support cue × injury contingency learning.

## Construct-validity gate-keeping summary

This is the section the postdoc should treat as load-bearing for v3 §5's pain-side content. It states, in compact form, what dissociations the project needs to satisfy before it can claim its results are pain-relevant rather than "generic RL modulation observed on a pain-flavoured testbed".

The minimum set is four dissociations, each pre-registrable on the current testbed plus the Phase-0 T/P split prerequisite:

1. **Channel-selective $\gamma$, not volume control** (P-Pain-1): the post-injury site-A gain rises on threat-relevant channels more than on threat-irrelevant ones. Dissociates the modulator from a 1-D Doya-NA temperature signal (which would broadcast uniformly). Without this, the modulator is doing inverse-temperature work and the "pain" framing is rhetorical — the project's claim retreats to "interoceptive neuromodulation produces hypervigilance-like survival benefits", which is L'Haridon & Cañamero 2023 at higher fidelity but not a pain-computation claim.

2. **Time-locked to the interoceptive injury signal, not to the threat input** (P-Pain-1 caveat): the gain shifts lag injury onset by $\le \min(5, 2\tau_{\text{mod}})$ steps regardless of whether predator presence has changed. Dissociates pain-computation from task-relevance-learning: any FiLM modulator trained on a task where threat channels happen to be diagnostic will learn selective gain — but only a pain-construct-valid modulator will be driven by the interoceptive injury inference, not by the threat input directly.

3. **Within-trial cross-domain coupling, $r \ge 0.3$ at lag $\le 5$** (`pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §2.2): the site-A gain shift, the site-B memory shift, and the site-C policy shift co-vary on the *same trial, same seed*. Dissociates "pain syndrome" (Vlaeyen-Linton joint perceptual-mnemonic-policy reweighting through a common modulator) from independent regulation. Under the v2 single-`mod_h` design this is by construction; under the T/P split it becomes testable on dissociable channels. Without it, the project is doing three separate gain stories at three sites, not pain.

4. **The dissociation from C1** (`pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §3): the modulated agent's behavioural signature differs from a trainable EMA-of-nociception baseline wired through the same A/B/C heads. C1 *can* reproduce a slow envelope and a uniform post-injury rise; C1 *cannot* reproduce channel-selective gain at different rates on different threat channels. The contrast metric $\Delta_t \gamma_{\mathcal{T}}^{(i)} - \Delta_t \gamma_{\mathcal{T}}^{(j)}$ between two threat channels with different injury-scaled noise profiles is the pre-registrable dissociator. Without C1 dissociation, the "pain computation" claim collapses to "pain smoothing".

If all four dissociations hold, "pain-like hypervigilance signature" is defensible at a *PAIN* or *Nature Mental Health* reviewer. If any one fails, v3 should retreat to the sober formulation in `pain_vs_nociception_construct.md` §4.4: "**interoceptive neuromodulation as a substrate for pain-like behaviour**" — honest about what is shown, no premature pain-computation commitment. The user does not have to choose now; the choice should follow the empirics. The four-dissociation gate is what makes the choice principled rather than rhetorical.

The R2 continual-learning empirical anchor (`nmn_r2_continual_h1b_h1c_confirmed_high_margin.md` — mod beats baseline ~25× the seed-noise floor on return-to-active) is — by itself — *not* evidence on any of these four dissociations. It is consistent with all four, but it is also consistent with all four being false and the modulator being a Doya-NA context detector. v3 should treat the R2 win as motivating the program, not as evidence the construct claim survives.

## Methodological notes

Predictions P-Pain-1, P-Pain-3, P-Pain-4, P-Pain-5 are **current-testbed-feasible** under the Phase-0 T/P split prerequisite. The per-modality $\gamma$ logging already exists or is the small senior-developer add-on flagged in `architecture_for_pain_computation.md` §1.5. The R2 continual schedule that produced the empirical anchor is sufficient for P-Pain-3.

Prediction P-Pain-2 (tonic prior-precision shift) is current-testbed-feasible **with one new probe trial type**: a post-recovery re-exposure of the predator cue without actual injury risk. This is small: a probe-trial harness that suppresses the injury-update for $n$ steps after recovery completes. Senior-developer scope.

Prediction P-Pain-6 (placebo analog) **requires testbed extension**: a learned cue × injury contingency in pre-training plus a cue-only test trial. This is not a small change — it adds a new associative-learning sub-task. v3 should flag it as a Phase-2-or-later direction, not as a near-term prediction. If the project wants to be the first to test predictive-coding-of-pain accounts under a forward-pass gain modulator (Gap 6 in `neuromodulatory_algorithms_predictions_synthesis.md` §5.6), this is the experiment, but the cost is real.

All predictions assume the **T/P split** is in place. Without it, P-Pain-2, P-Pain-3, P-Pain-4 are degenerate (the single tonic state forces correlated movement across knobs the construct demands be separable). This is the same prerequisite both `professor-neuromodulation` and `professor-rl-bayesian-dl` flagged in v1 §8.

The **rung set** the predictions need: rungs 1, 3, 5 from `architecture_for_pain_computation.md` §3.2. Rung 1 (injury without predators or food) is required for P-Pain-1 (clean pain-following-injury baseline without drive conflict); rung 3 (canonical heterogeneous noise + predators) is the home rung where all four dissociations are tested; rung 5 (slow injury dynamics) is required for P-Pain-2 and P-Pain-4 (tonic / outlasting signatures).
