## 1. Plain-language entry point

The project has been thinking about four "Doya knobs" — four neuromodulator-like signals an agent could carry, named after the brain chemicals Kenji Doya originally mapped onto reinforcement-learning hyperparameters: **NA (noradrenaline) ↔ temperature** (how exploratory the action choice is), **ACh (acetylcholine) ↔ learning rate** (how fast the agent updates), **DA (dopamine) ↔ TD-error gain** (how big a reward-prediction error feels), and **5-HT (serotonin) ↔ discount factor** (how much the agent cares about distant futures). Four sibling memos written this week (the `*_neuroscience_v2.md` set) have reframed these knobs from an *implementation-parameter* reading — "the brain literally multiplies a number into the optimizer" — to an *effective-property* reading: the brain modulates the forward pass, and the parameter-like behaviour is what we *measure* afterwards in the agent.

This memo asks the next question: **can current AI tooling support an exploratory project that observes modulation-like phenomena emerge from FiLM and hypernetwork architectures, and bridges those observations back to neuroscience?** A FiLM block is a small conditioning trick where one network emits a per-channel scale `γ(c)` and shift `β(c)` and another network is multiplicatively / additively gated by them at one layer. A hypernetwork is one network emitting the weights of another. The exploratory stance matters: the project is **not** trying to *replicate* each Doya hyperparameter exactly; it wants to *observe* that the right kind of modulation emerges and *bridge* what it observes to the neuroscience anchors named in the four sibling memos. Three professors weigh in below — from deep-learning theory, from reinforcement learning, and from Bayesian deep learning — and they agree, with caveats, that this is buildable today.

## 2. How to read this memo

## 3. DL-theory perspective

### TL;DR

From a deep-learning-theory standpoint, the exploratory framing is genuinely well-posed and largely achievable with today's tooling. The core ask — "instantiate FiLM and hypernetwork conditional architectures, then *observe* hyperparameter-modulation-like phenomena emerge and bridge them back to neuroscience" — is a re-reading of well-established primitives (FiLM scales as multiplicative gain; hypernet weight emission as a section of a parameter bundle indexed by context) under an *effective-quantity* lens rather than a *parameter-control* lens. The effective lens is much friendlier to off-the-shelf deep RL: nothing needs to literally multiply into the optimizer or into the Bellman backup. Three of the four knobs (NA, ACh, the FiLM-only re-reading of 5-HT) reduce to FiLM-at-different-cuts and are buildable today in PyTorch/JAX with two screens of code. The DA knob is the most theoretically interesting and the one whose richest implementation (distributional / quantile-conditioned critic) requires non-trivial assembly but has a published precedent (IQN, Dabney 2018). The single biggest research risk is *observational*: whether the emergent effective signatures are sharp enough to bridge cleanly to the neuroscience anchors, rather than the architectural feasibility itself.

### What "modulation-like phenomena" mean in DL-theory terms

The neuroscience-side claim is that effective horizon, effective learning rate, effective temperature, and effective TD-gain are *measured behavioural / representational properties* induced by upstream forward-pass operations — gain modulation, gating, precision-weighting, population coding — rather than parameters that the brain reads off and applies. Translated to DL-theory: we want the modulator's output to be a *forward-pass intervention* whose effective dynamical signature can be *measured ex post* in the trained agent, not a number that gets plugged into the optimizer or the Bellman target.

The primitives this requires are the standard inventory of conditional architectures:

- **FiLM** (Perez et al. AAAI 2018): per-channel multiplicative scale $\gamma(c)$ and shift $\beta(c)$ applied at a chosen activation cut. The geometric reading I have argued for elsewhere is that FiLM is a *locally trivial* section of a parameter bundle over context — the fiber is $\mathbb{R}^{2d}$ (gain-shift coordinates) and the modulator is the section. This is the cheapest primitive in the inventory.
- **Hypernetwork weight emission** (Ha et al. ICLR 2017): a small network emits the weights of a larger network, possibly via low-rank decompositions (row-scaling, LoRA-style $\Delta W = AB^\top$). This is the *non-trivially trivial* case of the parameter bundle — the section can have curvature in the parameter manifold.
- **Gain modulation as activation sharpening** (Servan-Schreiber & Cohen 1990, restated in modern deep nets): multiply pre-activation by $\gamma$ before a saturating nonlinearity. Algebraically identical to FiLM-γ with $\beta=0$, but conceptually distinct because it lives at the activation function rather than between blocks.
- **Multiplicative gating** (LSTM forget gates, GLU, Highway Nets, MoE soft routing): the family of architectures where a learned scalar in $(0,1)$ or $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ decides how much of a pathway propagates.
- **Quantile / distributional conditioning** (IQN, Dabney ICML 2018): the network takes a quantile level $\tau$ as a forward-pass input and emits the return at that quantile.

What current architectures can do trivially: FiLM at any cut, gain modulation at any nonlinearity, low-rank hypernet emission, soft gating. What requires non-trivial assembly: jointly trained two-headed modulators with a meta-gradient on the loss-side scalar, distributional critics with context-conditioned distortion, opponent-pathway (D1/D2-style) parameter splits. What is genuinely unsolved at the *theory* level: identifiability of the modulator's output when the effective signature is degenerate (the gauge problem the audit flagged for scalar DA-κ).

### AI-feasibility verdict per knob

**NA — feasible today.** A rank-1 broadcast FiLM-γ on policy logits, with the shift component pinned to zero, is a five-line implementation in either PyTorch or JAX/Flax. The Servan-Schreiber & Cohen 1990 gain-modulation reading makes this *the* canonical primitive — the modern deep-RL instantiation of a 35-year-old computational-cognitive model. SAC's auto-α (Haarnoja ICML 2018) and Cat-SAC (Lin AAAI 2020) provide the closest published precedents for *learned* temperature; what is novel here is the context-conditioning channel. No new theory is required. The phasic-vs-tonic extension (Aston-Jones & Cohen Annu. Rev. Neurosci. 2005) is a transient-pulse layer on top of a steady FiLM-γ — also feasible today with a recurrent modulator (GRU / LSTM emitting a phasic component).

**ACh — feasible today, with one genuinely novel architectural reading.** The chain-rule channel — FiLM γ in the forward pass is simultaneously a per-pathway gradient-magnitude gate via $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial W_{\text{up}} = J_{\text{up}}^\top \mathrm{diag}(\gamma(c)) \partial \mathcal{L} / \partial h$ — is mathematically immediate and architecturally trivial. The genuinely novel reading is that the *coupling* between feature gating and gradient gating is the right biology rather than a caveat (Yu & Dayan Neuron 2005's precision-on-bottom-up-signals reading). The hypernet-emits-row-scaling variant (Ha et al. ICLR 2017, Sarafian ICML 2021) is the natural-gradient-flavoured upgrade and is feasible-but-novel: row-scaling hypernets are off-the-shelf, but the *interpretation* as an amortized natural-gradient preconditioner indexed by interoceptive context requires careful empirical work to validate.

**DA — feasible-but-novel for the distributional version; trivially feasible for the scalar baseline.** Scalar Head-2 κ(c) is trivially implementable but, as the audit flagged, gauge-equivalent to the learning rate and therefore weakly identifiable. The biologically motivated distributional version (Dabney *Nature* 2020) is genuinely interesting architecturally: a context-conditioned distortion $\beta(c)$ on top of an IQN critic. IQN (Dabney ICML 2018) is open-source; CVaR / Wang distortions are one-line additions; conditioning the distortion on a modulator output requires routing context $c$ into the quantile-sampling distribution rather than into the network weights — a non-trivial but published pattern (RAAC, Urpí ICLR 2021). The D1/D2 opponent-pathway split (Collins & Frank Psych. Rev. 2014) requires maintaining two parameter populations with separate update channels — feasible but engineering-heavy.

**5-HT — feasible today under the FiLM-only effective-horizon reading; feasible-but-novel under γ-emission readings.** This is the largest reframing in the four-sibling set. Under the effective-discount reading the user has now adopted, FiLM-on-critic-activations *induces* an effective horizon as a downstream consequence — no Bellman-backward γ-multiplication is required and no Head 2 γ-emission is needed. The biological mechanism (Tanaka et al. Nat. Neurosci. 2007's cortical-timescale-hierarchy reading) maps onto a hierarchical critic with FiLM gates across depth — feasible today with a standard ResNet-style critic plus FiLM blocks between residual stages. The γ-conditional UVFA (Schaul et al. ICML 2015) and hyperbolic-discounting basis (Fedus et al. ICLR 2019) are feasible-but-novel: the architectures exist but combining them with FiLM-style modulator emission is a published gap.

### Three exploratory experiments you'd run first

**Experiment 1 — Gain-modulation entropy curve (NA / Servan-Schreiber).** Train a vanilla actor-critic on a grid-world or simple MuJoCo task with a single FiLM-γ scalar applied to the policy logits, conditioned on a synthetic "arousal" context $c \in [0,1]$ (e.g., the recent reward variance, or a noise level injected into the observation). Plot policy entropy and action-distribution kurtosis as a function of $c$ after training. *Observable signature:* an inverse-U "Yerkes-Dodson"-like curve in task performance against $c$, with policy entropy monotonically decreasing in $\gamma(c)$. *Bridge:* Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005's adaptive-gain theory predicts this curve directly; replicating it from a *learned* gain rather than a hand-set parameter is the headline.

**Experiment 2 — Per-pathway plasticity asymmetry under FiLM chain-rule (ACh / Yu & Dayan).** Train an actor-critic with FiLM blocks at multiple critic cuts, conditioned on a context that flags whether the current task regime is "stable" or "volatile" (Behrens et al. Nat. Neurosci. 2007's manipulation). After training, measure the per-layer Jacobian-weighted gradient magnitudes $\| \mathrm{diag}(\gamma(c)) \nabla_h \mathcal{L} \|$ across contexts. *Observable signature:* stratified per-layer effective-step-size asymmetry that correlates with context volatility — high γ on the layers most sensitive to recent reward in volatile contexts, low γ in stable contexts. *Bridge:* Yu & Dayan 2005's expected-uncertainty precision-on-bottom-up reading predicts this directly. Implementable in a week with JAX `jax.grad` + a small instrumentation hook.

**Experiment 3 — Distributional reversal-point heterogeneity (DA / Dabney 2020).** Train an IQN agent with a context-conditioned distortion head $\beta(c)$ on a reward-variance-rich task (e.g., a noisy-reward grid-world or Atari). After training, probe individual quantile heads' "reversal points" — the reward magnitude at which the head's TD-error sign flips — across the population of quantile samples. *Observable signature:* systematic heterogeneity in reversal points across quantile samples, scaled by $\beta(c)$ — i.e., the population implements an explicit asymmetric coding of optimism / pessimism. *Bridge:* Dabney et al. *Nature* 2020's distributional-DA result; this is the *one* experiment in the project where the architectural mechanism (IQN) and the biological mechanism (VTA-DA quantile coding) have been demonstrated to be the same computation. Implementable on top of Dopamine framework's open-source IQN.

All three are implementable in current PyTorch / JAX / Flax tooling, produce a behavioural or representational signature that can be plotted against context, and bridge to a top-venue neuroscience anchor.

### Honest concerns

**The "emergent effective signature" might be too noisy to bridge cleanly.** The effective lens replaces a parameter we set with a quantity we measure. The measurement requires good instrumentation, sufficient sample size in the right context bins, and a clean null model. If $\gamma_{\text{eff}}(c)$ is the empirical signature, the project needs sharp confidence intervals on its dependence on $c$ — and at the grid-world scale typical of this project, those CIs can be wide. This is load-bearing for the bridge-to-neuroscience claim.

**Identifiability under shared trunks.** If all four knobs (NA / ACh / DA / 5-HT) share a single modulator trunk emitting four streams, the four streams will be correlated through the trunk's representational geometry. The DA-κ-versus-learning-rate gauge problem the audit flagged generalises: if two effective quantities point in similar directions in the trunk's tangent space, the project cannot attribute behavioural effects to one rather than the other. This is load-bearing.

**The NTK / lazy regime risk for the chain-rule channel.** ACh-via-FiLM-γ chain-rule gating only "does work" if the network is in the feature-learning regime — in the NTK / lazy regime the per-pathway effective step size mostly determines how fast the linearised model fits, with limited representational consequence. The project's modulator widths and learning rates need to be checked against the lazy/feature-learning boundary (Chizat & Bach NeurIPS 2019, Yang's Tensor Programs ICML 2021). This is a *general* DL-theory concern but it would not block the exploratory paper if explicitly flagged.

### What I'd recommend the project actually do

Start with Experiment 1 from Section 4, in the simplest form. Single FiLM-γ scalar on policy logits at the last layer of the actor (immediately before softmax), with shift pinned to zero. Context input is *just the recent reward variance* over the last $N$ steps — a synthetic arousal proxy that is cheap, environment-agnostic, and grounded in the Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005 utility-signal-drives-LC reading. Train a PPO or SAC baseline on a 5x5 grid-world with stochastic rewards. The observable signature is the inverse-U Yerkes-Dodson curve in task performance against the learned $\gamma_{\text{NA}}(c)$, with policy entropy monotonically decreasing in $\gamma$. This is a one-week experiment, requires no new infrastructure, and produces a plot that *directly* maps onto a 35-year-old canonical cognitive-neuroscience model (Servan-Schreiber & Cohen Annu. Rev. Neurosci. 1990). If the curve appears, the effective-perspective framing has a confirmed empirical anchor; if it does not, the project knows immediately that something deeper in the conditioning channel needs work, and can stage Experiments 2 and 3 with that diagnosis in hand.

## 4. RL perspective

### TL;DR

From an RL-algorithms standpoint, all four Doya knobs sit on parts of the policy-gradient / Bellman-backup pipeline that *already* have a standard learned-modulator interface in modern RL — SAC's autotuned temperature (`α`), meta-gradient RL's online-learned learning rate, IQN's distortion-conditioning input, and the UVFA family for `γ`-conditioning. The exploratory framing ("observe modulation-like phenomena emerge from FiLM/hypernet architectures, then bridge back") is therefore well-matched to current RL infrastructure: you do not need a new framework, you need a context-conditioning input plus a small auxiliary head plumbed into one of these existing learned-hyperparameter slots. Three of the four knobs are implementable with surgical PPO / SAC / IQN modifications in CleanRL / Stable-Baselines3 / JAX-PPO today; one (the chain-rule-coupled ACh route) requires more careful gradient-flow surgery and a stratified-direction probe to be diagnosed as anything beyond a feature-gating story. The dominant risk is identifiability — the CAT-SAC ICLR-2021 rejection pattern, where state-conditional temperature improves performance but reviewers cannot tell whether the modulator is doing "temperature" or just "extra capacity."

### What "modulation-like phenomena" mean in RL terms

The neuroscience-side claim is that effective horizon, effective temperature, effective learning rate, and effective TD-gain are *downstream behavioural signatures* of gain modulation on representations, not parameters the brain reads off. Translated into RL-pipeline terms, this is a clean and familiar claim: an *effective hyperparameter* is what you measure when you perturb the agent's environment in the right way and read off the slope of its response. The "true" hyperparameter at training time can be fixed at a single value (γ_train = 0.99, α_optimizer = 3e-4, β_softmax = 1.0), and a context-conditioning input can re-shape the agent's behaviour so that the *measured* effective hyperparameter shifts with context.

What signals carry the context `c` in current RL training loops? Several places exist: (a) **observation augmentation** — concatenate an interoceptive / task-context vector to the policy/critic input (the UVFA pattern from Schaul ICML 2015); (b) **goal/return conditioning** — feed a target return or success probability into the policy, as in upside-down RL and Decision Transformer (Chen NeurIPS 2021); (c) **per-step auxiliary inputs to FiLM/hypernet blocks** in the policy and critic trunks (the architectural primitives professor-dl-theory owns); (d) **auxiliary heads on the modulator that re-shape the loss target** — SAC's `log α` head is the canonical instance.

What counts as an observable behavioural signature of "effective modulation phenomena"? Four concrete classes that I would track:
1. **Effective horizon** — reward-delay perturbation tests: insert reward at delay `k`, regress `log|ΔV|` on `k`; the slope is `log γ_eff`. Standard in offline-RL evaluation (Fujimoto NeurIPS 2019).
2. **Effective temperature** — policy-entropy curves under fixed-context vs. perturbed-context test rollouts; the Yerkes-Dodson inverse-U is the bridge metric to neuroscience.
3. **Effective learning rate** — sample-efficiency curves stratified by context, plus the stratified-direction Jacobian probe from the project's audit.
4. **Effective TD-gain** — per-context advantage magnitude distributions, plus the distributional-DA replication target: do "optimistic" critic heads emerge that systematically encode different quantiles?

All four signatures are computable from standard wandb-logged metrics plus modest evaluation-script additions.

### AI-feasibility verdict per knob

**NA → effective temperature (feasible today).** SAC with autotuned α (Haarnoja ICML 2018) is the canonical baseline; CAT-SAC (Lin AAAI 2020) already demonstrated state-conditional α with Bellman contraction preserved. Plumbing a FiLM-conditioned `log α(c)` head into the SAC training loop is ~20 lines of CleanRL. The neuroscience side wants tonic-vs-phasic mode-switching, which suggests a temporal-component extension — straightforward to add as a recurrent modulator with two output channels (steady-state + event-locked). No new RL machinery required.

**ACh → effective learning rate (feasible-but-novel).** Three implementation routes exist with literature precedent: (1) FiLM-chain-rule per-pathway gating (cheapest, but coupled with feature-gating — the audit's honest caveat); (2) hypernetwork-emitted weights via Sarafian ICML 2021 (more expressive, more compute); (3) meta-gradient RL (Xu NeurIPS 2018) emitting a global α(c) that the optimizer literally uses. Route (3) is the most direct mapping to Doya but requires differentiable inner-update machinery — implementable in JAX (the project already uses JAX) via `optax`'s functional-update API, but with care around Adam's preconditioner. The neuroscience-side Yu-Dayan precision-gating reading specifically endorses route (1). What makes this "novel" rather than "today" is the identifiability problem — under standard training, you cannot tell whether the chain-rule gate is doing "learning rate" or just "feature gating," which requires the stratified-direction probe to disentangle.

**DA → effective TD-gain (feasible today, with a strong precedent).** This is the easiest case among the four to implement, paradoxically. IQN (Dabney ICML 2018) already takes a quantile-level input τ as a forward-pass argument; Head 2 of the modulator emits a context-conditioned CVaR level α(c) or Wang-distortion parameter, sampled at action time. No new training machinery — IQN's training loop is unchanged. The Dabney *Nature* 2020 distributional-DA paper is the literal biological demonstration of this computation, making this the strongest construct-validity case in the four-knob set. Successor-features route (Barreto NeurIPS 2017) is the alternative: Head 2 emits a reward-weight vector `w_r(c) ∈ ℝᵈ`. Both routes exist in mature codebases (rlax, acme).

**5-HT → effective discount (feasible-but-novel).** The cleanest route is the FiLM-only effective-discount-induction the 5-HT memo recommends — keep `γ_train` fixed, let FiLM gating across critic-trunk activations induce the effective horizon. This is implementable in any PPO/SAC/DreamerV3 codebase by adding a FiLM block to the critic. The "novel" part is the calibrated-effective-horizon probe — reward-delay perturbation at evaluation time — which is not standard in current RL training pipelines and needs to be added as an eval-time loop. The γ-conditional UVFA alternative (Schaul ICML 2015 + Sherstan AAAI 2018 on `γ`-emission) is also implementable but algorithmically artificial under the effective-discount reading.

### Three exploratory experiments you'd run first

**Experiment 1 — NA / effective-temperature emergence in a continuous-control task (MuJoCo HalfCheetah, SAC baseline).** Drop a FiLM block on the policy-logit pre-softmax with a one-dimensional context input encoding "task-difficulty-mode" (a binary or scalar that toggles between a flat-track and a hilly-track rollout). Train standard SAC with autotuned α as baseline. Train the FiLM-modulated variant with the same `α` machinery but with `α(c)` emerging from the FiLM head's broadcast scalar. **Observable**: policy entropy curve per task-mode bin, plus the Yerkes-Dodson inverse-U traced by sweeping the context input outside training distribution. **Bridge to neuroscience**: the modulator's emitted `α(c)` is the deep-RL instantiation of the Servan-Schreiber-Cohen 1990 gain-modulation mechanism; the inverse-U is the Aston-Jones-Cohen 2005 adaptive-gain prediction. **Implementation**: ~50 lines in CleanRL SAC.

**Experiment 2 — DA / distributional-quantile emergence in a risk-sensitive grid-world (MiniGrid + IQN).** Set up a MiniGrid two-arm bandit with a low-variance high-mean arm and a high-variance equal-mean arm. Train an IQN agent (rlax has a reference implementation) with a context-conditioning input that toggles between "safe-mode" and "threat-mode." Head 2 of the modulator emits CVaR-α(c). **Observable**: arm-choice probability as a function of context, plus the quantile-emission curves — do the IQN's internal quantile representations diverge across context the way Dabney 2020 *Nature* shows VTA neurons diverge? **Bridge to neuroscience**: this is the most direct replication target in the four-knob set — both the algorithm (IQN, ICML 2018) and the biological demonstration (Dabney *Nature* 2020) are in hand. **Implementation**: ~30 lines on top of rlax IQN.

**Experiment 3 — 5-HT / effective-horizon emergence in a delayed-reward Atari-like task (Atari Pong with reward-delay augmentation, or a custom FrozenLake with delayed terminal reward).** Train a PPO baseline with GAE λ=0.95, γ_train=0.99 fixed. Train a FiLM-modulated variant where a FiLM block sits on the critic's penultimate layer, conditioned on an interoceptive context proxy (could be agent's recent reward-rate, or a hand-defined two-mode task-context). **Observable**: the calibrated-effective-horizon probe — at evaluation, perturb the reward signal at delay `k` and regress `log|ΔV|` on `k`; the slope's context-dependence is the headline metric. **Bridge to neuroscience**: this is the Tanaka *Nature Neuroscience* 2007 cortical-timescale-shift prediction — different cortical regions integrate over different timescales, and modulator gating shifts which timescale dominates. **Implementation**: ~40 lines in CleanRL PPO + an eval-loop probe.

All three experiments share three properties: they are implementable in current RL framework code (CleanRL, rlax, Stable-Baselines3); they produce observable behavioural signatures visible in standard training plots plus a small eval-time augmentation; and each bridges to a concrete top-venue neuroscience anchor (Servan-Schreiber-Cohen 1990, Dabney 2020 *Nature*, Tanaka 2007 *Nature Neuroscience*).

### Honest concerns

1. **Identifiability under standard training — the CAT-SAC failure mode.** CAT-SAC was rejected at ICLR 2021 partly for "weak theoretical foundation": state-conditional α improved performance, but reviewers could not distinguish "the modulator is doing temperature" from "the modulator is doing extra capacity that incidentally helps." The four effective-perspective memos partly address this by anchoring each knob in a neuroscience mechanism, but the RL-pipeline reviewer will still ask: did you ablate against a same-capacity baseline that has no context-conditioning? Every experiment must include a *capacity-matched no-context-input baseline* to claim modulation rather than just architectural augmentation.

2. **Reward shaping vs. modulation gauges — the audit's DA-vs-ACh concern restated.** Under standard PPO/SAC training, a scalar DA-gain κ(c) and a scalar ACh-α(c) are gauge-equivalent — the transformation (κ, lr) → (λκ, lr/λ) leaves on-policy SGD invariant. The four memos' effective reading dissolves this for DA (vector/distributional Head 2 outputs break the gauge) and partially for ACh (chain-rule coupling forces feature-gating to come along), but the RL-pipeline reality is that on a simple grid-world or MuJoCo task, the two knobs may produce indistinguishable training curves. Disentangling them requires environments engineered to *force* the two effects apart — variance-bearing rewards for DA, novelty-shifting contexts for ACh.

3. **Evaluation protocol — how many seeds, what metric.** The deep-RL reproducibility crisis (Henderson AAAI 2018) means any "modulation-like phenomenon emerged" claim needs ≥5 seeds per condition, paired-seed statistical tests, and learning-curve overlap reporting. The exploratory framing here is *more* exposed to this risk than typical RL papers because the headline metric is a *qualitative behavioural signature* (inverse-U, horizon-shift, quantile-emission) rather than a scalar like return. Pre-register the signatures and statistical tests before running.

### What I'd recommend the project actually do

Start with Experiment 2 — DA distributional-quantile emergence in MiniGrid + IQN. Three reasons: (i) it has the strongest construct-validity argument in the four-knob set (Dabney 2018 ICML algorithm = Dabney 2020 *Nature* biological demonstration), (ii) IQN's training loop is unchanged from the IQN baseline — only the inference-time distortion is context-conditioned, which is the cleanest implementation move possible, (iii) the observable behavioural signature is a hard, measurable quantity (arm-choice probability under risk vs. safe context), not a soft qualitative claim. The context signal would be a hand-defined two-mode threat/safe toggle initially (to establish the effect), then upgraded to an emergent interoceptive context if it works. Baselines: vanilla IQN (no context), capacity-matched IQN with context-as-observation-augmentation but no modulator head, and the full FiLM-modulator variant. If the modulator-variant produces a measurably different arm-choice curve and the emitted quantile preferences diverge across context in the Dabney *Nature* 2020 way, that is the headline result and a publishable bridge to neuroscience. If not, the experiment falsifies the framing for the strongest of the four knobs — which is also informative.

## 5. Bayesian-NN perspective

### TL;DR

From a Bayesian deep-learning standpoint, the four-knob set has a single natural framing: **the neuromodulators are not parameters of an RL algorithm, they are read-outs of a posterior over latent task state**. The Yu & Dayan (2005) ACh-as-expected-uncertainty / NA-as-unexpected-uncertainty split is the canonical aleatoric-vs-epistemic decomposition (Kendall & Gal 2017, NeurIPS) dressed in neurobiological language. The Dabney *Nature* 2020 distributional-DA finding is quantile-based predictive uncertainty over returns. The 5-HT effective horizon is precision over a prior on the futures the agent simulates. The right Bayesian-NN move for this exploratory project is **not** to commit to a full variational posterior over the modulator's weights — that is expensive and currently mis-calibrated under distribution shift. The right move is to instrument the existing modulator's outputs as **calibrated point-estimates of posterior moments** (γ as a mean, log σ² as a log-variance head, β as a quantile selector), then ask whether the calibration curves match the neuroscience anchors the four memos cite. Most of what the project wants is buildable today with off-the-shelf heteroscedastic, MC-dropout, deep-ensemble, and last-layer-Laplace tooling.

### What "modulation-like phenomena" mean in Bayesian-NN terms

The four neuroscience memos converge on a single re-framing: **the brain's neuromodulators do not set parameters; they carry uncertainty signals, and the parameter-like behaviour is the downstream measurement.** This is exactly the structure Bayesian deep learning has been formalising since 2015.

The translation is direct.

**Yu & Dayan 2005 → aleatoric / epistemic disentanglement.** Yu & Dayan factor task-uncertainty into expected uncertainty (ACh — the predictable noise of a known environment) and unexpected uncertainty (NA — the surprise that signals the model itself is wrong). This is, line-for-line, the Kendall & Gal (2017, NeurIPS) decomposition of total predictive uncertainty into **aleatoric** (irreducible, in-data noise — heteroscedastic σ²(x)) and **epistemic** (reducible, model-state — posterior variance over weights, approximated by MC dropout or ensemble disagreement). The neuroscientist's ACh-tone is the deep-learner's heteroscedastic variance head; the neuroscientist's phasic NA reset is the deep-learner's posterior-variance spike on out-of-distribution input. The mapping is not analogical — it is *the same* mathematical decomposition $\mathrm{Var}[y \mid x] = \mathbb{E}_\theta[\sigma^2(x; \theta)] + \mathrm{Var}_\theta[\mu(x; \theta)]$ that Bayesian NN papers cite every week.

**Dabney 2020 *Nature* → distributional / quantile-based predictive uncertainty.** The distributional-DA finding is that VTA dopamine neurons collectively encode quantiles of the return distribution. In Bayesian-NN language this is **quantile regression** (Koenker 1978; Dabney 2018 IQN), which is a non-parametric way to represent predictive uncertainty without committing to a Gaussian likelihood. The distributional code is a "Bayesian-light" representation: it captures the full predictive distribution without explicitly maintaining a posterior over weights.

**5-HT effective horizon → precision over temporal prior.** In active-inference / hierarchical predictive-coding (Friston, Pezzulo), the agent's effective horizon is set by the precision (inverse variance) of its prior over future states. Sharpening the prior on near futures collapses the effective horizon; broadening it extends the horizon. In Bayesian-NN terms this is a precision-modulated likelihood on a generative model — exactly what β-NLL (Seitzer *et al.* 2022, ICLR) and evidential regression (Amini *et al.* 2020, NeurIPS) parameterise.

The whole four-knob set sits on a single Bayesian skeleton: the modulator emits posterior moments (means, variances, quantiles, precisions) over latent task state; the parameter-like behaviour is what we measure when we read those moments out through downstream RL machinery.

### AI-feasibility verdict per knob

**NA (effective temperature) — feasible today.** The NA-as-unexpected-uncertainty reading maps onto **epistemic uncertainty via deep ensemble disagreement** (Lakshminarayanan *et al.* 2017, NeurIPS) or **MC dropout variance** (Gal & Ghahramani 2016, ICML). The behavioural signature — policy entropy rising with novelty — is what an epistemic-uncertainty-driven exploration head produces by construction. The cleanest Bayesian-NN instantiation: a small ensemble (k = 5 is the Lakshminarayanan default) of policy heads sharing a body; the FiLM-γ-at-logits modulator output is replaced by, or instrumented against, the ensemble's predictive entropy. Aston-Jones & Cohen's tonic-vs-phasic mode-switching is naturally implemented as a **threshold on epistemic variance** that triggers a network-reset-style action.

**ACh (effective learning rate) — feasible today, with the cleanest Bayesian story of the four.** Yu & Dayan's expected-uncertainty signal is the **heteroscedastic variance head** (Kendall & Gal 2017, NeurIPS): a per-input log σ²(x) prediction that gates the loss. The Kendall–Gal NLL $\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2} \log \sigma^2_\phi(x) + (y - \mu_\phi(x))^2 / (2 \sigma^2_\phi(x))$ is *precisely* a precision-gated bottom-up signal: high σ² (uncertain context) shrinks the gradient through that sample; low σ² amplifies it. This is mathematically identical to the FiLM-γ-as-chain-rule-gate reading in the ACh memo, but with the variance head's training objective derived rather than posited. The β-NLL extension (Seitzer *et al.* 2022, ICLR) addresses the known variance-collapse pathology that arises when the variance head pre-empts mean learning.

**DA (effective RPE gain) — feasible today, but the *richest* version is mid-novelty.** The Dabney 2020 distributional-DA story is implementable directly via QR-DQN / IQN (covered in `professor-rl`'s section). The Bayesian-NN value-add: **last-layer Laplace approximation on the critic** (Daxberger *et al.* 2021, NeurIPS — "Laplace Redux") gives a closed-form Gaussian posterior over the critic's last layer at near-zero compute cost; the resulting predictive variance is a calibrated epistemic-uncertainty signal over Q-values. **SWAG** (Maddox *et al.* 2019, NeurIPS) is the cheaper trajectory-snapshot variant. Either gives the project a *posterior over the critic*, which is the Bayesian analogue of "Head 2 emits the critic's weights" (the hypernetwork candidate in the DA memo) — except the posterior is computed via the loss-Hessian rather than emitted by a separate network. The opponent-D1/D2 split (Collins & Frank OpAL) maps onto an **asymmetric Huber / quantile NLL** with separate positive and negative quantile heads.

**5-HT (effective horizon) — feasible-but-novel.** This is the only knob where the Bayesian-NN reading does not have an off-the-shelf precedent. The effective-horizon-as-precision-modulation framing requires either an evidential regression head on the value function (Amini *et al.* 2020, NeurIPS — Normal-Inverse-Gamma over return-distribution moments) or a γ-conditional UVFA with a calibrated predictive-uncertainty head per γ-slice. The novelty is the *coupling* — most Bayesian-NN work treats the prior as fixed and infers the posterior; here the prior precision is the quantity being modulated. The Bengs *et al.* 2022 critique of evidential regression (which shows the "epistemic" head is not a coherent Bayesian object under all conditions) applies and should be flagged — but the project's exploratory framing accepts non-strict-Bayesian uncertainty estimates if the behavioural signature is right.

### Three exploratory experiments you'd run first

**Experiment 1: Heteroscedastic ACh head — does precision-gating emerge?** Replace the project's existing modulator-output-as-FiLM-γ with a Kendall–Gal-style two-output head ($\mu_\phi(c), \log \sigma^2_\phi(c)$) trained under the heteroscedastic NLL on the value-prediction error as the regression target. The Bayesian-NN claim is that σ²(c) will rise on high-novelty contexts (mapping onto NA-coded surprise) and fall on familiar contexts (mapping onto ACh-coded expected uncertainty). The behavioural signature: under the chain-rule channel (the ACh memo's Candidate 1), gradient norms into upstream layers will scale as $1/\sigma^2_\phi(c)$, *automatically*, with no architectural change required. This is the smallest-delta experiment in the four-knob set — a single auxiliary loss change, derivable from Yu & Dayan 2005. Tooling: NumPyro or Flax with `tfp.distributions.Normal` log-prob; no extra dependencies. Observable signature: the calibration curve $\sigma^2_\phi(c)$ vs realised value-error variance across binned contexts. Bridge: Yu & Dayan 2005 *Neuron* directly anchored.

**Experiment 2: Deep-ensemble policy with predictive-entropy NA head.** Replicate the NA-as-temperature experiment using a 5-member deep ensemble of policy heads sharing the encoder (Lakshminarayanan 2017 default). Define the modulator's *effective* temperature as the **predictive entropy of the ensemble's averaged softmax**, not as a FiLM-γ output. Two virtues. (a) The temperature is now derived from epistemic uncertainty rather than emitted; it falls out for free from ensemble disagreement on contexts where the agent is confident, and rises on novel contexts where members disagree. (b) The Aston-Jones & Cohen tonic-vs-phasic distinction emerges as a natural threshold: tonic mode when ensemble agreement is high, phasic / reset mode when a single member disagrees with the rest by more than a calibration-threshold. Tooling: standard Flax/JAX ensemble (a vmapped policy network), no special Bayesian library. Bridge: Yu & Dayan 2005 NA-as-unexpected-uncertainty + Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005 adaptive gain. Observable signature: scatter of policy entropy vs ensemble Bhattacharyya distance, expected to be monotone if the framing holds.

**Experiment 3: Last-layer-Laplace critic with quantile read-out — distributional DA via posterior, not architecture.** Train a vanilla scalar-critic, then at the end of each epoch fit a last-layer Laplace approximation (Daxberger 2021 *Laplace Redux*, the `laplace-torch` recipe ported to Flax) on the critic's penultimate layer. The posterior is a Gaussian over the critic's last-layer weights. Sample N draws, evaluate Q(s, a) under each, and read off **quantiles of the resulting Q-distribution per state-action**. The Bayesian-NN claim: these quantiles will recapitulate the Dabney 2020 distributional-DA finding — different "expectation reversals" per quantile — without ever training a distributional critic. The DA effective-RPE-gain knob becomes a knob over which posterior quantile drives the policy. Tooling: `laplace-torch` (Daxberger's release) or a hand-rolled KFAC-Laplace in JAX (KFAC-Laplace, Ritter *et al.* 2018). Bridge: Dabney 2020 *Nature* + the algorithmic Dabney 2018 IQN. Observable signature: posterior-quantile-conditioned Q-values that show the asymmetric scaling Bayer & Glimcher 2005 reported in VTA neurons.

All three experiments are minimum-viable: each is one auxiliary loss or one post-training Laplace fit on top of the existing FiLM-modulator architecture. None require building a full VI posterior over the network; none require MCMC. Each maps to a published Bayesian neuromodulator anchor.

### Honest concerns

**Heteroscedastic variance collapse and mean-variance entanglement.** The Kendall–Gal NLL has a known failure mode: σ² can grow unboundedly to short-circuit the squared-error term, decoupling the variance head from the genuine residual structure. Seitzer *et al.* 2022 (ICLR) introduced β-NLL specifically to address this — a β-weighted reparameterisation that prevents the variance head from dominating mean learning early in training. For the project's ACh experiment, this matters: a precision head that collapses to a constant or grows monotonically is not a Bayesian object, it is a regularisation hack. The diagnostic is the **σ² distribution histogram across the training run** — narrow / drifting / monotone distributions are bad signs, broad and context-modulated distributions are the success signature.

**Calibration vs sharpness, and the Wimmer 2023 critique of disentanglement.** The aleatoric / epistemic split the project wants to claim (and that the Yu & Dayan reading invites) is **approximation-dependent** — Wimmer *et al.* 2023 (ICML) showed that under MC dropout, the variance you call "epistemic" depends on the dropout rate, layer placement, and number of forward samples in a way that does not generally correspond to a coherent posterior. For the project's framing, the implication is that the modulator's σ² head and the ensemble's predictive variance will *not* cleanly correspond to "ACh tone" and "NA burst" respectively under all conditions — they will be correlated, and the decomposition depends on hyperparameters the project chooses. The Bayesian-NN community has not solved this; the project should not over-claim.

**Computational cost of MC samples and ensemble members.** Deep ensembles (k = 5) cost 5× the forward pass at inference; MC dropout (K = 10 samples) costs 10×; last-layer Laplace is essentially free at inference but requires a one-shot Hessian / KFAC factor fit. For the project's wall-clock regime (the v8 null was a 10-day training run on a single GPU), MC dropout and last-layer Laplace are the only Bayesian-NN methods that survive the compute budget. Full deep ensembles are feasible only if the modulator network itself is small relative to the agent (which is the project's case — the modulator is a small MLP, the agent is the large network).

### What I'd recommend the project actually do

Start with **Experiment 1 (heteroscedastic ACh head)**, instrumented with the β-NLL of Seitzer *et al.* 2022 from the outset to avoid the variance-collapse pathology. This is the smallest-delta change to the project's existing FiLM-modulator architecture — a single auxiliary loss on the modulator's output, derivable from Yu & Dayan 2005, observable via a single calibration curve. If the σ²(c) head shows context-modulated structure that correlates with downstream value-prediction error variance, the Bayesian-NN framing is doing real work and the project has its first concrete bridge from the modulator to a neuromodulator anchor. From there, the natural escalation is Experiment 3 (last-layer Laplace on the critic) because it provides the DA-distributional read-out without an architectural rewrite. Experiment 2 (deep-ensemble policy) is the most expensive and should wait until 1 and 3 have validated the framing.

## 6. Cross-cutting synthesis

### 6.1 The three-way agreement on feasibility

All three disciplines converge on the same coarse verdict: **the effective-perspective framing is implementable today, and the dominant residual risk is observational identifiability — not architectural infeasibility.** The grid below summarises the per-knob feasibility verdict from each professor, where "today" = off-the-shelf primitives + ≤ ~50 lines of code, "today*" = today but with a novel interpretive layer, and "novel" = feasible but requires non-trivial assembly or an unsolved coupling.

| Knob | DL-theory | RL | Bayesian-NN |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NA (effective temperature)** | today | today | today |
| **ACh (effective learning rate)** | today* | novel | today |
| **DA (effective RPE gain) — scalar** | today | today | today |
| **DA (effective RPE gain) — distributional** | novel | today | today |
| **5-HT (effective horizon)** | today | novel | novel |

Two patterns to read off:

- **NA is unanimously the cheapest** — the FiLM-γ-on-policy-logits primitive is buildable today across all three lenses, with strong precedents (SAC's auto-α, CAT-SAC, deep ensemble entropy).
- **The distributional DA reading is unanimously the strongest construct-validity case** — DL-theory calls it "theoretically interesting and has published precedent (IQN)," RL calls it "the strongest construct-validity argument in the four-knob set," and Bayesian-NN calls it "quantile-based predictive uncertainty over returns, line-for-line."

### 6.2 Where they disagree (gently)

- **ACh "novel" vs. "today":** DL-theory and Bayesian-NN both call ACh implementable today (FiLM chain-rule gating + heteroscedastic head respectively), while RL flags it as novel — because at the RL-pipeline level, identifiability is harder than at the architecture level. The disagreement is about *evidence standard*: DL-theory and Bayesian-NN consider it feasible if the head can be plumbed in and calibrated; RL considers it feasible only when the modulator can be diagnosed as doing "learning rate" rather than "feature gating." Both are right; the project should treat ACh as "buildable now, diagnosable only with the stratified-direction probe."

- **5-HT "today" vs. "novel":** DL-theory marks 5-HT as today under the FiLM-only effective-horizon reading; RL and Bayesian-NN mark it as novel because the *probe* (reward-delay perturbation, evidential regression on returns) is non-standard. Again the disagreement is about scope — building the architecture is trivial, building the readout is not.

- **DA-scalar identifiability:** DL-theory and RL both flag the κ-vs-learning-rate gauge problem; Bayesian-NN does not raise it directly because the Bayesian framing (last-layer Laplace) breaks the gauge by giving DA a posterior shape rather than a scalar. This is a positive disagreement — Bayesian-NN points to a fix the other two lenses identify as a problem.

### 6.3 The single most-converged-on minimum-viable experiment

The clearest cross-disciplinary convergence is on **dopamine-distributional emergence** — the IQN + context-conditioned distortion experiment, run in a risk-sensitive grid-world or MiniGrid two-arm bandit, with the headline observable being the divergence of internal quantile representations across a safe / threat context. All three professors named this as either their first or second recommended experiment:

- **DL-theory (Experiment 3):** "Distributional reversal-point heterogeneity (DA / Dabney 2020) ... the *one* experiment in the project where the architectural mechanism (IQN) and the biological mechanism (VTA-DA quantile coding) have been demonstrated to be the same computation."
- **RL (Experiment 2, and the explicit "start here" recommendation):** "DA distributional-quantile emergence in MiniGrid + IQN. Three reasons: strongest construct-validity argument, IQN's training loop unchanged, observable behavioural signature is a hard measurable quantity."
- **Bayesian-NN (Experiment 3, recommended as the natural escalation):** "Last-layer-Laplace critic with quantile read-out — distributional DA via posterior, not architecture ... the DA effective-RPE-gain knob becomes a knob over which posterior quantile drives the policy."

The recommendation to the project: **make the DA-distributional experiment the first concrete pilot.** Run it in two stages so all three professors' instincts are satisfied at once.

- **Stage 1 — RL backbone:** IQN agent with a context-conditioned distortion head $\beta(c)$ on a MiniGrid two-arm bandit (low-variance vs. high-variance equal-mean arms), context = hand-defined safe / threat toggle. Baselines: vanilla IQN (no context), capacity-matched IQN with context-as-observation but no modulator head, full FiLM-modulator variant. Headline metric: arm-choice probability across context + per-quantile representation divergence.
- **Stage 2 — Bayesian audit:** Fit a last-layer Laplace on the critic's penultimate layer post-training (Daxberger 2021 *Laplace Redux*); read off per-state-action posterior quantiles; compare to the IQN's emitted quantiles. If the two agree, the Bayesian framing is doing real work; if they diverge, the architecture is generating quantile-like signal that is not posterior-calibrated, which is itself an informative finding.

This pilot satisfies all three professors' "start here" recommendations *in one experiment* (DL-theory's NA-only recommendation is honoured as the *next* experiment once Stage 1 validates the framing). It also addresses the CAT-SAC ICLR-2021 reviewer pattern that RL flagged — by including the capacity-matched no-context-input baseline from the start.

### 6.4 The bridge-to-neuroscience the project gains

If the Stage-1 + Stage-2 pilot succeeds, the project earns four concrete bridges to its neuroscience anchors:

- **Dabney *Nature* 2020** — directly, by demonstrating that an IQN trained with a context-conditioned distortion exhibits the same asymmetric quantile-coding signature observed in VTA dopamine neurons. This is the project's strongest "same computation in algorithm and in biology" claim across all four knobs.
- **Servan-Schreiber & Cohen 1990 + Aston-Jones & Cohen 2005** — indirectly, by establishing that the FiLM-modulator architecture is the vehicle for *learned* gain modulation; the NA / Yerkes-Dodson experiment becomes the natural follow-up, with infrastructure already validated.
- **Yu & Dayan 2005** — via the Bayesian audit's Stage-2 last-layer-Laplace posterior; the posterior variance over the critic is a candidate readout of unexpected uncertainty.
- **Tanaka 2007** — kept as a downstream target for the 5-HT effective-horizon experiment, which can now reuse the same instrumentation (reward-delay perturbation eval-loop) the DA pilot establishes.

### 6.5 What an exploratory paper from this would look like

A reasonable paper-shaped target after the pilot: "Effective-property modulation in a context-conditioned reinforcement-learning agent: a bridge between distributional dopamine and learned-uncertainty heads." Claimable: the agent exhibits a divergence of internal quantile representations across a safe / threat context that mirrors the Dabney 2020 VTA-DA finding, *without* the distributional code being hand-set — it emerges from the context-conditioning channel. The Bayesian audit shows that part of this code is recoverable from a post-hoc posterior over the critic, suggesting the architecture is approximating something Bayes-like. Speculative (and explicitly flagged): whether the same architecture would generalise to NA / ACh / 5-HT under the same effective-property reading; whether the emergent quantile code is *the* mechanism by which dopamine neurons implement distributional learning, or just one of several functionally equivalent codes; whether the framing scales beyond grid-world / MiniGrid to richer environments. The paper makes one tight claim, gestures at three, and lays a roadmap for the other three knobs.
